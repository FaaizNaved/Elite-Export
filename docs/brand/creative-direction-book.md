## Purpose

The Brand Bible established who Elite Export is. This book establishes **what
that looks like** — and, more often, what it refuses to look like.

It contains no designs. It specifies no measurement, no colour, no typeface, no
grid, no duration. Those belong to Phase 6, and a book that contained them would
expire the moment a fashion changed. What follows is the reasoning a designer
must hold before making a single mark, so that two designers working ten years
apart on different media arrive at compatible work without having met.

### The rule of this book

Every visual decision in it answers one question:

> **Which principle from the Brand Bible does this express?**

Each chapter carries a **Traces to** line. A chapter that cannot name its
parent has no right to exist and should be struck. If anything here contradicts
the Brand Bible, **the Brand Bible wins and this book is wrong.**

### What this book is not

Not a UI document. Not a design system. Not a Figma file. Not a moodboard. Not a
style guide. Not a plan for any page.

It is the philosophy behind every visual decision, written to outlast the
medium it is first applied to.

# PART I — VISUAL PHILOSOPHY

## 1. Why we must not look like a leather manufacturer

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §20 Competitive position · D3 Nothing that could
belong to a competitor · §1 Brand Myth

Our category inherited its appearance from two ancestors, and both are wrong for
us.

The first is the **trade catalogue**: dense grids, small photographs, item codes,
a specification beside every picture, everything on the page at once. It is an
honest form, and it treats the company as a supplier of interchangeable units.
It answers *what can I order*. It cannot answer *who are you*.

The second is the **equestrian lifestyle brand**: golden light, a horse in a
field, a rider silhouetted, leather photographed as an object of desire. It is a
consumer form aimed at a person buying for pleasure. Our buyer is not buying for
pleasure. They are buying under obligation, for their employer, and they will be
asked to justify the choice.

Both ancestors produce work that looks **for sale**. We must look **at work**.

That distinction generates almost everything in this book. A company that looks
for sale is asking; a company that looks at work is simply continuing, and the
visitor is a guest in the middle of something already happening. The second
position is enormously stronger, and it is unavailable to any competitor who
does not own a floor to be at work in.

### 1.1 Our actual ancestors

We should be visually descended from forms that document rather than sell:

| Form | What we take from it |
| --- | --- |
| **Industrial architecture photography** | The building as subject; scale conveyed without drama; interest in structure |
| **Scientific and technical documentation** | Neutrality; the caption as specification; nothing exaggerated for effect |
| **Editorial journalism** | The photograph carrying the argument; text serving the image; pacing across a spread |
| **Museum and auction catalogues** | Objects given room; provenance stated plainly; confidence in silence |
| **Process and archive photography** | Work in progress valued over finished glamour |

None of these forms flatters its subject. All of them are believed.

### 1.2 The test

> If our work were placed beside eight competitors with all names removed, a
> stranger should be able to identify which one owns a factory.

Not which one is prettiest. Which one is real.

## 2. The visual emotions that must exist

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §7.1 Emotions the brand must create

The Brand Bible names six emotions. This chapter states the **visual condition**
that produces each. An emotion without a mechanism is a wish.

### Recognition

*Produced by:* the unglamorous specific. A scrap bin. A worn bench edge. A hand
that is not a model's hand. Marks on a hide that a lifestyle brand would have
removed.

Recognition is never produced by a beautiful image. It is produced by an image
somebody outside a factory would not have thought to take.

### Reassurance

*Produced by:* continuity. The same building appearing again and again from
different angles until the viewer accepts, without deciding to, that the place
exists. One photograph is a claim. Twenty photographs of the same rooms are a
building.

### Calm

*Produced by:* the absence of competing focal points. Calm is not a colour or a
softness — it is the experience of never being asked to choose what to look at.

### Competence

*Produced by:* precision at the edges. Alignment that holds. Consistent treatment
across unlike things. A viewer reads care in the small joins long before they
read the words, and they read carelessness faster still.

### Permanence

*Produced by:* the absence of anything datable. See [§8](#8-permanence-made-visible).

### Respect

*Produced by:* visible restraint. The empty half of a spread. The photograph
published without a caption. Respect is generated by what we decline to do, and
it is the only emotion on this list that cannot be manufactured by addition.

## 3. The visual emotions that must never exist

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §7.2 Emotions that must never appear

| Emotion | How it enters visually | The correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Excitement** | Anything that moves on arrival; high-chroma accents competing with content; multiple attention-seeking elements in one field | Nothing announces itself. The page is already underway when you arrive. |
| **Urgency** | Contrasting call-to-action colour; repeated actions; anything that looks like a countdown or a badge | One action, in the ordinary voice of the page |
| **Aspiration as fantasy** | Golden hour; horses in landscape; models; shallow-focus glamour; the product styled as an object of desire | Working light, working hands, the object in the condition it leaves in |
| **Flattery** | Soft focus; flattering angles; imagery that idealises the buyer's life | Neutral documentation |
| **Defensiveness** | Comparison tables; "why choose us" grids; badge walls; anything that argues | Evidence stated once, without a rebuttal attached |
| **Novelty** | Any technique traceable to the year it was made | See [§8](#8-permanence-made-visible) |

> **The badge wall is the category's signature anxiety.** A row of certification
> marks, awards and membership logos says *please believe me* in the most
> literal possible way. Our certifications appear — once, at the point in the
> narrative they have been earned — and never as a strip of graphics.

## 4. How a buyer should experience the brand

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §8 The emotional journey

The Bible defines seven stages. This chapter defines the **visual condition** of
each. It is not a storyboard: no page, no sequence of images, no order of
sections is specified here. That is Phase 3.

| Stage | The visual condition that produces it |
| --- | --- |
| **1 Arrival** | One image, large enough to be an environment rather than a picture. Almost no text. Nothing moving. The visitor is inside a place before they have read a word. |
| **2 Curiosity** | Structural unfamiliarity — a field organised unlike the category's. Felt, not noticed. If a visitor consciously admires the design, this stage has failed upward into vanity. |
| **3 Recognition** | The unglamorous detail, given enough size to be examined. **This is the stage the entire visual system exists to serve.** Every other decision may be traded; this one may not. |
| **4 Trust** | External marks presented plainly and late, in the ordinary voice of the page. |
| **5 Confidence** | Systematic treatment — evidence of process visible in how the work itself is organised. See [§9](#9-repeatability-made-visible). |
| **6 Respect** | Emptiness held where the category would fill. The visible decision not to push. |
| **7 Conversation** | One action, quiet, unmistakable, with nothing competing beside it. |

### 4.1 The rule of the hinge

Recognition (Stage 3) is load-bearing. When a visual decision must be traded
against another, **the decision that better serves Recognition wins**, without
exception. A beautiful arrival that delays or dilutes Recognition is a net loss.

## 5. Restraint, made visible

**Traces to:** Brand DNA — Restraint · P2 Restraint over decoration · D2 Silence
is a material

Restraint is the hardest of the five to review, because it is invisible by
construction. Nobody notices the element that was never added. This makes it the
first thing to erode and the last thing to be missed.

### 5.1 What restraint looks like

- **Fewer things, larger.** The instinct to add a second element should be
  redirected into enlarging the first.
- **One action, not a choice.** A choice between two actions is not generosity.
  It is the transfer of a decision we should have made ourselves.
- **Emptiness where a claim would fit.** The most confident space on any surface
  is the one a competitor would have filled.
- **No element that explains another element.** A label under an obvious thing is
  an apology.
- **The willingness to publish something silent.** A photograph with nothing to
  say gets no caption.

### 5.2 What restraint is not

It is not minimalism. Minimalism is a style with its own decorations — thin
lines, vast margins, tiny type, and a great deal of self-regard. Restraint is
not a look at all; it is an editing behaviour. A dense page can be restrained if
everything on it is load-bearing. A sparse page can be indulgent if its
emptiness is decorative.

> **The test.** Remove any element. If nothing is lost, it was decoration and it
> stays removed. Apply until removal costs something. That is the finished state.

## 6. Evidence, made visible

**Traces to:** Brand DNA — Evidence · P1 Evidence over claims · D1 Photography
carries the argument · §19 The Facts Register

### 6.1 Evidence must be examinable

This is the central visual consequence of the whole brand, and it produces the
most rules:

> **A photograph too small to be examined is not evidence. It is decoration.**

There is a threshold — different in every medium, unspecifiable here — below
which an image stops functioning as proof and becomes ornament. Above it, a
viewer can inspect the grain, the stitch, the state of the bench, and satisfy
themselves. Below it, they can only recognise a shape and take our word for what
it contains, which is precisely the position the brand exists to avoid.

**Therefore: the size of an image is not an aesthetic decision. It is an
evidential one.** A thumbnail of the factory floor makes a weaker claim than no
photograph at all, because it asserts and withholds simultaneously.

### 6.2 The visual grammar of evidence

- **Show the mechanism, not the symbol.** The press cutting, not an icon of a
  press. The report, not a checkmark.
- **Leave the unflattering in.** Scuffs, offcuts, an untidy bench. Evidence that
  has been tidied is testimony that has been coached.
- **Prefer the wider frame when it contains proof.** A hand alone could be
  anywhere. A hand with a working floor behind it is located.
- **Caption as specification, never as sentiment** (Brand Bible §12.4).
- **Never retouch a natural mark.** Removing a scar from a hide is a fabricated
  fact (Brand Bible §17.4).

### 6.3 Decoration is not forbidden — it is reclassified

We do not ban beauty. We ban beauty that does no work. Every handsome thing in
this brand should be handsome **because** it is doing its job well: a large
photograph is beautiful and is proof; generous space is beautiful and is
composure. Beauty is a by-product here, never a brief.

## 7. Ownership, made visible

**Traces to:** Brand DNA — Ownership · P3 Ownership is the story · §1 Brand Myth

Ownership is the only one of the five a competitor cannot decide to adopt, which
makes it the most valuable and the most under-used.

### 7.1 Interiors, not exteriors

An exterior photograph of a building proves nothing — anyone can stand outside
anything. **Ownership is proved from the inside**, repeatedly, from angles that
could only be obtained by someone who works there.

### 7.2 Continuity of place

The single most persuasive visual device available to this brand is **the same
room appearing again**. A window recognised from an earlier frame. A floor
marking that recurs. A bench seen first in the background of one image and in
the foreground of another.

This cannot be faked with a library, cannot be assembled from suppliers, and
cannot be produced by a company that does not own the space. It requires no
explanation and is almost never consciously noticed — which is why it works.

### 7.3 The camera belongs to a colleague

Height, distance and angle all carry authorship. A camera at working height,
close enough to be present and not so close as to stage, reads as somebody who
belongs there. A camera looking down reads as an inspector. A camera on a crane
reads as an advertisement.

### 7.4 First person about the work

Visually, this means our own equipment, our own people, our own light — never
generic substitutes. It is better to have fewer images, all ours, than a
complete set half of which is borrowed. One borrowed frame invalidates the
collection (Brand Bible §16.1).

## 8. Permanence, made visible

**Traces to:** Brand DNA — Permanence · P5 Built to outlast a trend

### 8.1 The dating test

> Could a knowledgeable person name the decade this was made in?

If yes, it fails. Not because the decade is bad, but because the visitor's
relationship with us is measured in decades and anything that visibly belongs to
one of them implies we belong to it too.

### 8.2 What dates work

Almost always a **technique**, rarely a **structure**:

| Dates quickly | Survives |
| --- | --- |
| Processing fashions and colour treatments | Neutral, faithful rendering |
| Effects that imitate a device or medium | Materials and forms photographed as they are |
| Whatever is currently novel in interaction | Composition, proportion, hierarchy |
| Illustration styles | Photography of real things |
| Anything designed to be noticed as design | Anything designed to be read |

Structure ages slowly. A well-proportioned field of type and image is as correct
now as it was in a Swiss annual report in 1961 and will be in 2045. Technique
ages in single-digit years.

**Therefore: invest in structure, spend nothing on technique.**

### 8.3 Materials over effects

Where the brand needs richness, it comes from what is being photographed — the
leather, the brass, the light on a worn surface — and never from a treatment
applied afterwards. Real material is permanent. Applied effect is fashion.

## 9. Repeatability, made visible

**Traces to:** Brand DNA — Repeatability · §17.3 Variation in the material,
never in the work · D7 Hierarchy communicates importance

A buyer's central fear is that piece four hundred will not match the sample. We
cannot answer that with a sentence. We answer it by **being visibly systematic**.

### 9.1 The system must be perceptible

A viewer moving through our work should develop, without articulating it, an
expectation of how the next thing will be treated — and should be right. That
correctness is the argument. A company whose own materials are inconsistent is
asserting a process discipline it visibly does not have.

> **Surprise belongs to content. Never to treatment.**
>
> What we show may astonish. How we show it must never deviate. A new product, a
> new market, a new decade — the treatment holds.

### 9.2 What repeatability looks like

- One rendering of light across every image, whoever shot it and whenever
- The same logic of crop and framing applied to unlike subjects
- Consistent structural approach across a family of surfaces
- The same treatment for the same class of information, everywhere, forever

### 9.3 The relationship to material variation

Brand Bible §17.3 draws the line and it is repeated here because it is the
easiest to blur in visual work: **variation in the material is honest; variation
in the work is failure.** Two hides may differ in tone and both be photographed
truthfully. Two photographs may not differ in grade. The first is the subject
being itself; the second is us being careless.

## 10. What visual confidence means

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §9 Brand personality · §16.4 Luxury as the absence of
anxiety

Confidence and anxiety produce visibly different work, and a viewer detects the
difference before they can name it. Almost every failure in this category is an
anxiety symptom.

| Anxiety does this | Confidence does this |
| --- | --- |
| Says it three ways in case one lands | Says it once |
| Offers several actions | Offers one |
| Fills the space | Leaves it |
| Announces itself on arrival | Is already underway |
| Decorates the claim | States the claim |
| Explains the photograph | Publishes the photograph |
| Adds a badge | Omits the badge |
| Asks to be believed | Allows itself to be checked |

### 10.1 The single diagnostic

> **Does this surface behave as though it needs the visitor?**

A surface that needs the visitor pushes: it repeats, it decorates, it offers
alternatives, it moves to attract. A surface that does not need the visitor
simply presents, completely and once, and is content to be examined or not.

We are the second. Not because we do not want the order — we do — but because a
manufacturer who appears to need this order appears to have too few, and a buyer
notices that instantly.

## 11. Photography dominance and immersion

**Traces to:** D1 Photography carries the argument · §16.1 Photography philosophy

### 11.1 Dominance is structural, not proportional

"Photography dominates" does not mean a percentage. It means the **photograph is
the primary carrier of meaning and everything else is annotation.** The
consequence is a reversal of the ordinary working method: one does not design a
field and then find an image for it. One establishes what the photograph is
saying and then decides what, if anything, needs to be added.

If a surface would still make its argument with the words removed, the
photography is doing its job. If it would collapse, the photograph was
illustrating rather than proving, and either the image or the claim is wrong.

### 11.2 Immersion

Immersion is the difference between **looking at a picture** and **being in a
place**. It is produced by extent — an image that continues past the edge of the
field implies a world that continues past the edge of the frame. An image
bounded on all sides is an object on a page; an image running out of the field
is a view.

This is why our photography prefers to run out of the field, and why the
occasional bounded image should be a deliberate change of register rather than a
default.

### 11.3 One image at a time

Two photographs of equal weight beside each other halve each other. The eye
compares instead of examining, and comparison is a shopping behaviour. Where
several images must coexist, they must be **unequal** — one leads, the others
support — so the eye is directed rather than asked to choose.

### 11.4 What photography must never become

- A texture behind text
- A shape to balance a composition
- A mood-setter with no informational content
- Interchangeable with a different photograph of the same subject

The last is the sharpest test. **If any competent photograph of the same subject
would serve equally, the image is decoration.** Evidence is specific or it is
nothing.

## 12. Silence, whitespace and negative space

**Traces to:** D2 Silence is a material · §16.2 Whitespace philosophy

### 12.1 Three words for one behaviour

*Whitespace* is the compositional term, *negative space* the formal one,
*silence* the emotional one. They describe the same act: **choosing not to
occupy.** This book prefers *silence*, because it correctly implies that the
alternative is noise rather than merely fullness.

### 12.2 Silence is allocated, not left over

Space that remains after everything has been placed is not silence. It is
residue, and it reads as such. Silence is decided first, protected, and never
reclaimed to accommodate a late addition. When something new must be added and
there is no room, the correct response is to remove something, not to compress
the space.

### 12.3 Silence is proportional to importance

The most important element on any surface should have the most space around it.
This is the primary way importance is communicated without enlargement, and it
is more reliable than size, because a large thing in a crowd is still in a crowd.

### 12.4 The held moment

Every extended experience must contain at least one passage that asks nothing —
no claim, no action, no reading. This is not a rest for the visitor's benefit.
It is a **structural device**: an emotional shift cannot register while demand
is continuous. The claim that follows silence lands harder than the same claim
in sequence with others.

A held moment that shares its field with anything else is not held. **Isolation
is what makes it work**, and a held moment interrupted by a persistent element —
a bar, an offer, a control — is not a held moment. It is an advertisement with a
picture behind it.

### 12.5 Density is permitted

Silence is not uniform emptiness. Passages of real density are legitimate and
necessary — a technical page, a specification, a record. What is forbidden is
**undifferentiated** density: everything equally full, everywhere, with no
release. Density earns its place by contrast with silence, and vice versa.

## 13. Scale and hierarchy

**Traces to:** D7 Hierarchy communicates importance · D4 One idea per view ·
§16.3 Typography philosophy

### 13.1 Scale is meaning

Size is not a matter of taste. **Size is the statement of what matters**, made
before a word is read, and it is the fastest signal available. A viewer entering
any surface should know within a second which thing on it is most important —
and if everything is the same size, the honest reading is that nothing is.

Uniform scale is the most common failure of otherwise careful work, because
consistency feels like discipline. It is not discipline. It is abdication: the
designer has declined to say what matters and left the visitor to work it out.

### 13.2 Hierarchy must be steep

Adjacent levels of importance must be **unmistakably** different. A hierarchy of
subtle differences is not perceived as a hierarchy at all — it is perceived as
inconsistency, which is worse than flatness because it suggests carelessness.

Where two things genuinely matter equally, they should not be adjacent. Where
they must be adjacent, one must be given priority anyway. **The refusal to
prioritise is not neutrality; it is a decision to confuse.**

### 13.3 Not everything deserves a level

A hierarchy with many levels is not a hierarchy. Few, strongly differentiated
levels serve the reader; many, weakly differentiated levels serve only the
person who could not decide.

### 13.4 The two voices

Brand Bible §16.3 assigns the roles: **the serif is the company speaking; the
sans is the record.** The visual consequence is that the two are not
interchangeable and should never be used to create variety. Variety is not a
goal. Each voice appears where its role is being performed and nowhere else.

Figures belong permanently to the record. A number set as a statement becomes
rhetoric; a number set as a record can be checked.

## 14. Contrast, light and shadow

**Traces to:** §16.1 Photography philosophy · Brand DNA — Evidence

### 14.1 Contrast is a tool of attention, not of drama

Contrast exists to direct the eye and to separate levels of importance. It does
not exist to create excitement. Any contrast that draws attention to itself
rather than to something has been misused.

The most valuable contrast available to this brand is not tonal but
**structural**: photograph against silence, density against emptiness, a held
moment against a working one. These are stronger than any treatment and they
never date.

### 14.2 Working light

Our light is the light the work is actually done in — directional, from a real
source, at a real time of day. It is not studio light, which reads as commercial
and implies the subject was brought somewhere to be photographed. It is not
dramatic light, which reads as theatre and implies interpretation.

Working light is credible precisely because it is imperfect: it falls unevenly,
it changes across a room, and it produces exposures a studio would reject. Those
imperfections are the evidence that nothing was arranged.

### 14.3 Shadow keeps its detail

A shadow that falls to pure black is a place where information has been
discarded. For a brand whose position is *you may examine us*, discarded
information is a small contradiction — and enough small contradictions are
eventually felt as one large one.

**Shadow conceals nothing. It is where the light happens to be less.** This is
a philosophical position before it is a technical one, and it survives into every
medium: a printed page, a projected film, a lit stand.

### 14.4 Light and the passage of time

Light is the primary means by which a brand communicates *when*. Anything lit as
a current fashion lights things will be legible as that fashion within a decade.
Faithful, unremarkable, correctly exposed light is invisible and therefore
permanent. See [§8](#8-permanence-made-visible).

## 15. Composition, balance and asymmetry

**Traces to:** D5 Asymmetry with intent · D4 One idea per view

### 15.1 Symmetry is the signature of a template

Centred, mirrored, evenly divided arrangements read as defaults because they are
what happens when no decision is made. They are also what every template
produces. Symmetry says *this was arranged by a system*; asymmetry says *this was
arranged by a person who decided*.

### 15.2 When asymmetry, when symmetry

| Use asymmetry | Use symmetry |
| --- | --- |
| Almost always | For a single, final statement standing alone |
| Where one element leads and others follow | Where the content is genuinely a pair or a set of equals |
| Where the eye should travel | Where stillness is the point |
| Where a photograph must dominate its field | Where a formal register is deliberately invoked |

**Symmetry must be earned by an argument.** Asymmetry is the resting state; a
symmetrical arrangement should be able to explain itself.

### 15.3 Balance without symmetry

Asymmetry is not imbalance. A large quiet mass balances a small dense one; a
photograph balances a passage of silence. Balance is a matter of **visual
weight**, not of mirrored position, and an asymmetric field that feels stable is
the clearest evidence of an author at work.

### 15.4 Editorial composition

The editorial tradition organises a field as **facing pages** rather than as a
stack: elements relate across the field, the eye crosses as well as descends, and
the unit of design is the spread rather than the row. This is the correct
tradition for us because it treats every surface as part of a continuous
publication rather than as an independent module.

The practical consequence: **elements should be composed against each other, not
merely placed in sequence.** A photograph and the text beside it are one
composition, not two components that happen to be adjacent.

## 16. Focus and eye movement

**Traces to:** D4 One idea per view · §10 Visual confidence

### 16.1 One entry point

Every field has exactly one place the eye lands first, and it should be the
place we chose. If two elements compete, the visitor performs a small act of
arbitration before they begin — and that arbitration is experienced as friction,
even though it is never noticed.

### 16.2 A path, not a scatter

After entry, the eye should have somewhere obvious to go, and after that,
somewhere else. Composition is the design of that route. Elements arranged with
equal claim on attention produce a scatter, and a scatter is read as a form to be
processed rather than a story to be followed.

### 16.3 Focus is created by subtraction

The reliable way to make something the focus is not to strengthen it but to
weaken or remove everything around it. This is [restraint](#5-restraint-made-visible)
expressed as composition, and it is the most frequently ignored technique in the
category, where focus is usually attempted by adding emphasis to the thing
already present.

### 16.4 Never two peaks in one field

Two elements of maximum weight in one view cancel: the visitor sees a busy
surface rather than either element. **One peak per field. The next peak waits.**

---

## 17. Rhythm, pacing, sequencing and transitions

**Traces to:** §15 Storytelling principles · §8 The emotional journey ·
Brand DNA — Repeatability

### 17.1 Uniform intensity is not a story

An experience held at one level is not perceived as sustained interest. It is
perceived as **monotony**, and it produces disengagement more reliably than
anything weak, because there is no variation to re-catch attention. Everything
important, presented continuously, is experienced as nothing important.

This is the most common structural failure in careful work: each part is good,
the parts are similar, and the whole is flat.

### 17.2 Peaks require valleys

A peak is not a property of an element. **It is a relationship between an element
and its neighbours.** A powerful passage surrounded by other powerful passages is
not a peak; it is more of the same. To create emphasis, weaken what is beside it.

The corollary governs planning: **quiet passages are not filler between the good
parts. They are what make the good parts good**, and they should be defended in
review with the same conviction as the peaks.

### 17.3 Sequence carries argument

The order of images is an argument, independent of the images themselves. Wide
then close reads as *here is the place, now look at this detail in it*. Close
then wide reads as *here is a detail — and it is inside all of this*. The frames
are the same; the claim is different.

Two rules follow:

- **A sequence should establish before it examines.** A detail with no
  established context is decoration.
- **A sequence should resolve.** Ending on an unresolved detail leaves the viewer
  holding a question we chose to give them.

### 17.4 Transitions between chapters

A change of chapter must be **felt before it is understood**. The available
means, in order of strength:

1. **Silence** — the strongest and most often forgotten
2. **A change of tonal register** — light to dark, or the reverse
3. **A change of density** — dense to open
4. **A change of scale** — the largest element of the new chapter unlike the last

What must not carry a transition: a rule, a border, a label announcing a new
section, or a change of background applied for variety. These name the transition
instead of producing it, and a named transition is an admission that the
structure did not communicate.

### 17.5 Chapters are unequal by design

Equal-length chapters produce a metronome. The chapter carrying the most weight
should occupy the most extent — and a viewer's sense of what matters is formed
more by how long they spend somewhere than by anything they are told.

### 17.6 Immersion is built by rhythm, not by effects

A visitor becomes absorbed when their expectations are formed and then
selectively met — which is a property of pacing. No treatment, no motion and no
device produces immersion. Only structure does.

## 18. Texture and material honesty

**Traces to:** D6 Material is felt, not seen · §17 Material philosophy

### 18.1 Below the threshold

Any reference to our materials — grain, stitch, tooling, hardware — belongs at
the **threshold of perception**. It should register as a quality of the surface
rather than as a pattern that can be described. The moment a visitor can identify
the motif, it has become decoration and it must be reduced or removed.

The correct experience is that a visitor who was asked afterwards whether there
was a texture would not be certain.

### 18.2 Never over photography

Texture applied over a photograph competes with the evidence and, worse, implies
the photograph was insufficient. Photography is the argument; nothing is applied
on top of an argument.

### 18.3 Never adjacent to itself

Two textured passages in sequence read as a pattern library rather than as
material. Between any two, plain ground.

### 18.4 Material honesty in representation

Brand Bible §17.4 governs how we describe materials. Its visual equivalent:

- We do not simulate a material we do not use.
- We do not render an artificial surface to imply a natural one.
- We do not use imagery of leather we did not work.
- We do not retouch a hide toward uniformity — uniformity in this material is a
  warning sign, and manufacturing it in post-production is manufacturing a fact.

### 18.5 Richness comes from the subject

Where the work needs warmth or depth, it comes from what is photographed — the
material itself, under real light. It never comes from an applied treatment. This
is the same principle as [§8.3](#8-permanence-made-visible): real material is
permanent, applied effect is fashion.

## 19. What a page is for

**Traces to:** §21 How every page supports the brand · §8 The emotional journey

Brand Bible §21 assigns every page a **buyer question** and a **brand
obligation**. This Part adds the layer that governs visual work: what the page
must make a visitor **feel**, what must never happen on it, and where it sits in
the emotional journey.

No page is designed here. No layout, no order of elements, no structure. Only
the emotional brief a designer must satisfy.

### 19.1 Three rules that apply to every page

**Every page must be capable of being someone's first.** A visitor may arrive
anywhere. No page may assume the visitor has read another, and every page must
independently establish that a real factory is speaking.

**Every page must answer its question before it elaborates.** The obligation is
discharged in the first view, not after scrolling.

**Every page ends knowing what comes next.** Not with a demand — with a plain
statement of the next reasonable step.

## 21. The laws of the field

**Traces to:** D1–D8 · §10 Visual confidence · Brand DNA throughout

No layouts are specified. These are the laws any layout must satisfy, in any
medium, on any surface.

---

**L1 — One dominant idea per view.**
Whatever occupies a visitor's field at any moment presents one thing. A view
containing two ideas contains none.
*Traces to: D4*

**L2 — Photography leads; everything else annotates.**
The image is decided first. Type, rules and space are arranged in response to
it, never the reverse.
*Traces to: D1*

**L3 — No competing focal points.**
One entry point per field. Where a second element has equal weight, reduce it or
separate them.
*Traces to: D4, §16.4*

**L4 — One emotional peak at a time.**
Peaks are separated by passages of lower intensity. Two adjacent peaks are one
plateau.
*Traces to: §17.2*

**L5 — Asymmetry is the resting state.**
Symmetry must be justified by an argument, not chosen by default.
*Traces to: D5*

**L6 — Space is allocated before content is placed.**
Silence is designed, protected, and never surrendered to a late addition.
*Traces to: D2, §12.2*

**L7 — Importance is expressed by space before it is expressed by size.**
The most important thing has the most room around it.
*Traces to: §12.3*

**L8 — Hierarchy is steep or absent.**
Adjacent levels differ unmistakably. Subtle differences read as inconsistency,
not as sophistication.
*Traces to: D7, §13.2*

**L9 — Interfaces recede.**
Borders, boxes, containers, shadows and controls are the least valuable marks
available. Every one must justify itself against doing nothing.
*Traces to: D8*

**L10 — Nothing repeats within a field.**
A fact, a mark or a call appears once. Repetition within one view reads as
insecurity.
*Traces to: §10, §10.1 tone rules*

**L11 — Elements are composed against each other, not merely stacked.**
The unit of design is the field, not the row.
*Traces to: §15.4*

**L12 — Density and silence alternate.**
Neither uniform emptiness nor uniform fullness. Contrast between them is the
primary structural instrument.
*Traces to: §12.5, §17.1*

**L13 — Every field must survive the removal test.**
Remove any element. If nothing is lost, it stays removed.
*Traces to: P2, §5.2*

**L14 — Structure carries the meaning; treatment carries none.**
Any effect that could be removed without changing the argument should be.
*Traces to: P5, §8.2*

**L15 — No surface may depend on another to make sense.**
A visitor may arrive anywhere.
*Traces to: §19.1*

## 22. Chapters, tension and breathing

**Traces to:** §17 Rhythm and pacing · §15 Storytelling principles

### 22.1 How a chapter begins

A chapter begins with a **change**, not with a label. The visitor should feel
they have entered something new before they read anything that tells them so.

Two failures to avoid:

- **The announced opening** — a rule, a background change or a heading doing the
  work that structure should have done.
- **The identical opening** — every chapter beginning in the same manner. When
  openings are uniform, chapters stop being felt as chapters and become sections
  in a stack. Openings should vary in kind, not only in content.

### 22.2 How a chapter ends

A chapter ends by **releasing**, not by concluding. A summary is unnecessary; the
visitor was there. The most reliable ending is a reduction in demand: the last
thing asked of the reader is smaller than the thing before it, and then there is
space.

A chapter that ends on its loudest element has not ended. It has stopped.

### 22.3 How tension is created

Tension is expectation without immediate resolution. The legitimate means:

- **Withholding** — establishing a subject and delaying the detail
- **Scale disparity** — something very large adjacent to something very small
- **Interruption of an established rhythm** — a pattern the visitor has learned,
  broken once, deliberately
- **Silence before a claim** — the pause that makes the next statement land

Tension is never created by urgency, motion, colour or contrast for its own
sake. Those produce agitation, which is the opposite emotional register
(Brand Bible §7.2).

### 22.4 How silence is created

Silence requires **isolation**, not merely emptiness. Space around an element
that still shares its field with a persistent control, a label or an offer is not
silence — it is padding. To create silence, the field must be genuinely cleared
of demand, including persistent elements that follow the visitor.

### 22.5 How breathing works

Breathing is the alternation of demand and release across an extended
experience. It has three requirements:

1. **Regularity enough to be felt** — release arrives often enough that the
   visitor's system learns to expect it
2. **Irregularity enough not to be mechanical** — but never on a fixed interval,
   which produces a metronome
3. **Proportion** — the longest and most demanding passage earns the longest
   release after it

A brand that never breathes is exhausting regardless of the quality of its parts.
A brand that breathes correctly can hold attention through material that would
otherwise be too long — which is precisely what our manufacturing story requires.

# PART V — DESIGN DECISION FRAMEWORK

## 23. The visual gate

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §23.1 The universal gate

Brand Bible §23 governs whether a piece of work should exist. This chapter
governs whether a **visual element** should exist within it. The Bible's gate
runs first and is not repeated here; passing it is assumed.

Every proposed element passes these four, in order. **Failure at any gate stops
the element. Later gates do not rescue it.**

**Gate 1 — Necessity.**
Remove it. Is anything lost?
→ *No:* **Stop.** It is decoration.

**Gate 2 — Attribution.**
Which of Ownership, Evidence, Repeatability, Restraint, Permanence does it serve?
→ *None, or it contradicts one:* **Stop.**

**Gate 3 — Distinctiveness.**
Do three competitors have something like it?
→ *Yes:* **Stop.** (D3)

**Gate 4 — Duration.**
Could a knowledgeable person date it?
→ *Yes:* **Stop.** (§8.1)

> **The default answer to "may I add this?" is no.** Not out of severity, but
> arithmetic: a brand whose position is restraint accumulates its identity by
> subtraction, and every addition spends a little of it.

## 24. Element decision trees

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §23 Decision framework · D8 Interfaces recede · P2
Restraint over decoration · §19 The Facts Register

Each tree assumes Brand Bible §23 and [§23](#23-the-visual-gate) above have been
passed.

### 24.1 An icon

```
Is it decorative, or does it perform a function?
├─ Decorative ────────────────► REJECT
└─ Functional
   │
   Is it wayfinding or status (a telephone, an address, an outbound link)?
   ├─ No ─────────────────────► REJECT. Icons do not illustrate concepts here.
   └─ Yes
      │
      Would a word do the same job?
      ├─ Yes ─────────────────► Use the word
      └─ No ──────────────────► PROCEED at the smallest useful presence
```

**Standing rejection:** any grid of icons representing qualities, benefits or
services. This is the category's most recognisable construction (§25.3) and it
is unavailable to us permanently.

### 24.2 A gradient

```
What is it for?
├─ Decoration ────────────────► REJECT
├─ Brand expression ──────────► REJECT
├─ Depth or dimensionality ───► REJECT
└─ Legibility of text over a photograph
   │
   Can the photograph be chosen or cropped so none is needed?
   ├─ Yes ─────────────────────► Do that instead
   └─ No
      │
      Is it neutral — a darkening, not a colour?
      ├─ No ───────────────────► REJECT
      └─ Yes
         │
         Is it as weak as legibility permits?
         ├─ No ────────────────► Reduce until it is
         └─ Yes ───────────────► PROCEED
```

A scrim is a legibility instrument. It is never a design element, and it is never
tinted.

### 24.3 A border or rule

```
What is it doing?
├─ Making a box ──────────────► REJECT (D8)
├─ Decorating ────────────────► REJECT
└─ Separating two things that would otherwise be confused
   │
   Would space alone separate them?
   ├─ Yes ─────────────────────► Use space (L7)
   └─ No
      │
      Is it a hairline, at the lightest weight that reads?
      ├─ No ───────────────────► Reduce it
      └─ Yes ──────────────────► PROCEED
```

### 24.4 A card

```
Default position: REJECT.

A card is a container, and a container is interface (D8). It exists to make
unlike things look alike, which is a convenience for the builder and a loss for
the reader.

Is the content genuinely a set of equivalent, independent items that will be
compared?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT. Compose the content directly.
└─ Yes
   │
   Can it be expressed as a ruled list or a plain sequence instead?
   ├─ Yes ─────────────────────► Do that
   └─ No ──────────────────────► PROCEED without border, surface or shadow —
                                  which is to say, not as a card at all
```

### 24.5 A button

```
Is there already an action in this field?
├─ Yes ───────────────────────► REJECT. One action per field (L1).
└─ No
   │
   Is this the single most important thing a visitor could do here?
   ├─ No ──────────────────────► Make it a plain link
   └─ Yes
      │
      Does its label describe the literal action?
      ├─ No ───────────────────► Rewrite it (Brand Bible §12.3)
      └─ Yes ──────────────────► PROCEED
```

**Never:** a secondary button of equal weight beside a primary one. A choice is
a decision we failed to make (§5.1).

### 24.6 A hover or interaction state

```
Does it communicate that something is interactive, or does it reward interaction?
├─ Rewards ───────────────────► REJECT. Delight is not our register.
└─ Communicates
   │
   Is the change the smallest that reads?
   ├─ No ──────────────────────► Reduce it
   └─ Yes
      │
      Do all parts of the element change on one clock?
      ├─ No ───────────────────► Unify them. A gesture arriving in pieces
      │                           reads as unfinished.
      └─ Yes ──────────────────► PROCEED (timing is Phase 5)
```

### 24.7 An animation

```
Does it exist to guide attention or to reveal?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT
└─ Yes
   │
   Would the work be worse without it?
   ├─ No ──────────────────────► REJECT
   └─ Yes
      │
      Does anything bounce, spring, overshoot, or repeat indefinitely?
      ├─ Yes ──────────────────► REJECT. Nothing this brand makes is springy,
      │                           and perpetual motion is a nervous habit.
      └─ No
         │
         Does it delay access to information?
         ├─ Yes ───────────────► REJECT
         └─ No ────────────────► PROCEED (specification is Phase 5)
```

### 24.8 A badge or label

```
Default position: REJECT.

Is it a certification mark?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT
└─ Yes
   │
   Has the narrative already earned it (Brand Bible §15.2)?
   ├─ No ──────────────────────► Move it later, or remove it
   └─ Yes
      │
      Is it appearing as a graphic among other graphics?
      ├─ Yes ──────────────────► REJECT. A badge wall is the category's
      │                           signature anxiety (§3).
      └─ No ───────────────────► PROCEED, stated plainly, once
```

### 24.9 A statistic

```
Is it Confirmed under the Facts Register (Brand Bible §19.3)?
├─ No ────────────────────────► STOP. Not a design question.
└─ Yes
   │
   Does it already appear elsewhere in this experience?
   ├─ Yes ──────────────────────► REJECT. One fact, stated once (L10).
   └─ No
      │
      Is it presented as a record, or as rhetoric?
      ├─ Rhetoric (oversized, animated, celebrated) ──► Reduce to a record
      └─ Record ──────────────────────────────────────► PROCEED
```

A number that is celebrated stops being evidence and becomes a claim, which
reverses its entire purpose.

### 24.10 A divider

```
Is it marking a chapter boundary?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT. Use space.
└─ Yes
   │
   Has the structure already communicated the boundary (§17.4)?
   ├─ Yes ──────────────────────► REJECT. The divider is redundant, and a
   │                               named transition admits the structure failed.
   └─ No ───────────────────────► Reconsider the structure first.
                                   A divider is the last resort, not the tool.
```

### 24.11 A background texture

```
Is it over photography?
├─ Yes ───────────────────────► REJECT (§18.2)
└─ No
   │
   Is it adjacent to another textured passage?
   ├─ Yes ─────────────────────► REJECT (§18.3)
   └─ No
      │
      Could a visitor describe the motif after seeing it?
      ├─ Yes ──────────────────► Too strong. Reduce until they could not (D6).
      └─ No
         │
         Does it reference a material we actually work?
         ├─ No ────────────────► REJECT (§18.4)
         └─ Yes ───────────────► PROCEED
```

### 24.12 Anything not listed

Apply [§23](#23-the-visual-gate). If it survives all four gates and no tree
above covers it, it may proceed — and this chapter should be extended in the next
version to cover it.

# PART VI — FAILURE MODES

---

## 25. Everything that would damage the brand

**Traces to:** the whole Brand Bible

Each failure below carries its **early warning sign** — the thing visible in
review *before* the failure is fully built. Catching a failure at the warning
stage costs a conversation; catching it later costs the work.

### 25.1 Category failures — looking like something else

| Failure | What it looks like | Breaks | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Looking like a storefront** | Item counts, tiles, prices, "shop" behaviour, comparison | Ownership | The word "browse" appears |
| **Looking like a framework default** | Even grids, uniform cards, generic containers, default proportions | Permanence | Everything is the same width |
| **Looking like software marketing** | Feature grids, benefit icons, contrasting CTA, testimonial carousel, gradient hero | All five | Any three-column row of equal blocks |
| **Looking like a trade catalogue** | Dense listings, small images, specification beside every item | Evidence | Photographs shrinking to fit more of them |

### 25.2 Register failures — the right idea, wrong voice

| Failure | Why it is fatal | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Luxurious instead of trustworthy** | Luxury signalling reads as consumer marketing and invites scepticism from a professional buyer. We are not selling pleasure. | Styling appears; products are arranged rather than photographed |
| **Handcrafted instead of industrial** | Undersells capacity and breaks the central tension (§4.2). A buyer needing four thousand pieces reads "artisan" as "cannot" | Only hands and tools appear; no machine, no room, no scale |
| **Futuristic** | Contradicts Permanence and reads as compensating for a lack of history | Anything blue-lit, anything rendered, any "innovation" language |
| **Trendy** | Dates the relationship | A technique appears that was not in use five years ago |
| **Precious** | Over-refinement reads as a design exercise, not a factory | Extreme delicacy; type too small to read comfortably |

### 25.3 Density failures — too much of a good thing

| Failure | Threshold | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Too many components** | The visitor perceives assembly rather than authorship | Two surfaces share a construction that was not deliberately shared |
| **Too many cards** | Any | A container is added to make unlike things look alike |
| **Too many icons** | Any decorative use | An icon is chosen to represent a concept |
| **Too much gold** | Accent becomes theme | The accent appears more than once in a single field |
| **Too much explanation** | The reader is told what they can see | A caption describes the obvious content of a photograph |
| **Too much UI** | Interface is noticed | Borders and containers outnumber photographs |
| **Too much symmetry** | Any unearned use | A centred arrangement nobody argued for |
| **Too many calls to action** | More than one per field | A "secondary" action of equal weight |

### 25.4 Role failures — elements doing the wrong job

**Photography becoming decoration.**
The most serious failure available to this brand, because photography is the
evidence and evidence is the whole position. It occurs when an image is chosen
for how it looks rather than what it proves, when it is reduced below the size at
which it can be examined (§6.1), or when it is placed to balance a composition.
*Warning sign:* an image could be swapped for a different photograph of the same
subject without loss.

**Typography losing hierarchy.**
Every heading at one size, every level weakly differentiated. The reader learns
nothing about importance and the work reads as a stack.
*Warning sign:* two consecutive chapters open at the same weight.

**The interface becoming louder than the company.**
Controls, containers, transitions and states accumulate until the visitor is
aware of a website rather than a factory.
*Warning sign:* anyone describes the work as "slick".

**Silence becoming padding.**
Emptiness that is uniform rather than allocated. It reads as an absence of
content, not composure.
*Warning sign:* space distributed evenly rather than proportionally to
importance.

**Motion becoming performance.**
Movement that is admired. Any motion the visitor notices as motion has failed.
*Warning sign:* a reviewer says an animation is "nice".

### 25.5 Integrity failures — unrecoverable

These do not damage the brand. They end its argument, and no later work
repairs them.

| Failure | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **A stock photograph, anywhere, at any size** | The claim of ownership becomes unverifiable, and the visitor has no way to know which other images are ours |
| **A retouched hide** | A fabricated fact (§18.4) |
| **An unverified statistic** | Directly contradicts the one sentence the brand is built on |
| **An unattributed testimonial** | The category's most common lie, and the one a professional buyer is most alert to |
| **A borrowed layout from a competitor** | Forfeits the only position we hold |

> Every failure in §25.5 is a Brand Bible violation before it is a design
> failure, and no visual judgement may overrule it.

## 26. The universal review

**Traces to:** the whole document

Every piece of work in any medium passes this review before release. It is
pass/fail. **Any single failure returns the work**; a review is not scored.

### 26.1 The one question

If there is time for only one:

> **Is there anything here that exists only because it looks good?**

If yes, the work fails. Remove it and review again.

### 26.2 The full review

**Distinctiveness**
- [ ] Could this belong to another manufacturer?
- [ ] With all names removed, would a stranger identify the one that owns a factory?
- [ ] Does anything here appear on three competitor sites?

**Evidence**
- [ ] Is the photography proving something, or illustrating something?
- [ ] Is every image large enough to be examined?
- [ ] Could any image be swapped for a different photograph of the same subject without loss?
- [ ] Is every fact traceable to the confirmed company record?

**Restraint**
- [ ] Does this feel quieter than the competitors it will be compared with?
- [ ] Remove any element — is anything lost?
- [ ] Is there more than one action in any single field?
- [ ] Is anything stated twice?

**Ownership**
- [ ] Is every image ours, from inside our building?
- [ ] Would a visitor accept, without being told, that this place exists?

**Repeatability**
- [ ] Is the treatment consistent with everything else the brand has made?
- [ ] Would a stranger be able to predict how the next piece is treated?

**Permanence**
- [ ] Would this still look correct in ten years?
- [ ] Could a knowledgeable person name the decade this was made in?

**Hierarchy and composition**
- [ ] Is it obvious what matters most, within a second?
- [ ] Is there exactly one entry point for the eye?
- [ ] Is there a peak, and does it have a valley beside it?

**Interface**
- [ ] Is the interface disappearing?
- [ ] Do containers and borders outnumber photographs?

**Emotional**
- [ ] Would a buyer trust this company more because of this?
- [ ] Does anything here behave as though it needs the visitor?
- [ ] Is there a moment that asks nothing?

### 26.3 On failure

Return the work with the failed question named. Do not negotiate the question,
and do not accept an exception on grounds of deadline, client preference or
beauty. **A brand built on restraint is destroyed by exceptions, not by
mistakes** — a mistake is corrected once, and an exception becomes the new
standard.

If the work fails and the reviewer believes the question is wrong, the correct
remedy is to amend this book in a new version, not to pass the work.

## Appendix A — Reference Canon

**Traces to:** §1 Why we must not look like a leather manufacturer · §8
Permanence · D3 Nothing that could belong to a competitor

### How to use this canon

These references exist for **calibration, not inspiration**. They are here to
settle arguments about register — how loud, how warm, how still, how plain — not
to be looked at before working.

Three rules govern their use:

1. **Study the register, never the technique.** Adopting a reference's technique
   imports its date (§8.2). Adopting its register imports nothing.
2. **D3 still applies.** A reference being admirable does not make its devices
   available to us. If three competitors already do a version of it, it is
   closed regardless of its pedigree.
3. **Nothing here is a target.** We are not trying to look like any of these. We
   are trying to be recognisable as the same kind of seriousness.

## Appendix B — Red Flag Checklist

**Traces to:** §23 The visual gate · §25 Failure modes · §26.3 On failure

One page. Keep it where reviews happen.

A red flag is **not a veto**. It is an instruction to stop and return to the
document before proceeding. Most requests below are made in good faith by
capable people, and most have a legitimate underlying concern — the checklist
exists to find the real concern rather than to implement the first solution
proposed for it.

---

### B.1 Things you will hear

| If someone says | STOP, because | Read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "It needs more animation." | Motion guides or reveals. If the work is not worse without it, it is decoration. | §24.7 |
| "We need more icons." | Icons here are wayfinding only. An icon grid is the category's signature construction. | §24.1 |
| "We should add another CTA." | A choice between two actions is a decision we failed to make. | §24.5, L1 |
| "We should add gradients." | A scrim is a legibility instrument, never a design element, never tinted. | §24.2 |
| "Let's put it in a card." | A container makes unlike things look alike. That is convenience for the builder and loss for the reader. | §24.4 |
| "It feels empty." | Emptiness is the design. Establish whether it is unallocated (a real fault) or allocated (the point). | §12.2, §12.3 |
| "It's a bit boring." | Ask whether it is monotonous or quiet. Monotony is a real failure; quiet is the brand. | §17.1, §17.2 |
| "Let's make it pop." | Excitement is a forbidden register. | §3 |
| "Can we make the logo bigger?" | Confidence is not expressed by volume. | §10 |
| "Our competitors all have this." | That is a reason to reject it, not to add it. | D3, §23 Gate 3 |
| "Let's add a testimonial." | Real, attributable, permissioned — or omitted. There is no third option. | Bible §19.5 |
| "Can we add some stats?" | Only if Confirmed, only once, and only as a record rather than as rhetoric. | §24.9, Bible §19 |
| "Add a trust strip / badges." | A badge wall is the category's signature anxiety. | §24.8, §3 |
| "Just use a stock photo for now." | Unrecoverable. A single borrowed frame makes every other image unverifiable. | §25.5 |
| "Shrink the images so more fits above the fold." | Below a threshold a photograph stops being evidence and becomes decoration. | §6.1 |
| "Tighten the spacing to fit this in." | Space is allocated first and never surrendered. Remove something instead. | L6 |
| "Make the headings consistent." | Uniform scale is not discipline. It is a refusal to say what matters. | §13.1 |
| "It should feel more premium." | Premium is a thing to be, never a thing to signal. Ask what specifically feels cheap. | Bible §10.3, §25.2 |
| "Make it more modern." | Anything identifiably of a decade dates the relationship. | §8.1 |

### B.4 The three that end the argument

These are not design disagreements. They are Brand Bible violations, and no
visual judgement, deadline or client preference may overrule them.

> **A stock photograph, anywhere, at any size.**
> **A retouched hide.**
> **An unverified number.**

Any one of them contradicts the single sentence the company is built on. If you
are being asked for one of these, the conversation is no longer about design.

---

### B.5 When you are overruled

Record it. A decision made against this book is not automatically wrong — the
book may be incomplete — but it must be visible, because the danger is not the
first exception. It is the second, which cites the first.

> A brand built on restraint is destroyed by exceptions, not by mistakes. A
> mistake is corrected once; an exception becomes the standard. (§26.3)

If the exception was right, amend the book in the next version. If it was wrong,
the record is how it gets caught.
