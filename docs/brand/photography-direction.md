## Purpose

The Brand Bible established **who Elite Export is.** The Creative Direction Book
established **what that looks like.** The Documentary Storyboard established
**how the brand tells stories.** This book establishes **how the brand makes
photographs** — the operating manual for anyone standing on the floor with a
camera, and for anyone deciding afterwards whether what they came back with may
be published.

It is not a shot list. It specifies no equipment, no setting, no arrangement of
images and no quantity of them. Those expire. What follows is the reasoning a
photographer must hold before making a single exposure, so that two photographers
working fifteen years apart, who have never met, return work that belongs in the
same library.

### The rule of this book

Every chapter carries a **Traces to** line naming its parent in Phase 1, 2 or 3.
A chapter that cannot name its parent has no right to exist and should be struck.
Where this book conflicts with the Documentary Storyboard, that book wins. Where
it conflicts with the Creative Direction Book, that book wins. Where it conflicts
with the Brand Bible, **the Brand Bible wins and this book is wrong.**

### The boundary with Phase 2

The Creative Direction Book already governs photography — but it governs
photography's **behaviour inside a composed surface**: that it dominates, that it
must be large enough to be examined, that it may not become texture behind text.

This book governs the **making of the photograph itself**: where the photographer
stands, what is in the frame, how it is lit by the room, what may be done to it
afterwards, and whether it may exist at all.

Where a Phase 2 rule has a Phase 4 counterpart, this book states the counterpart
and cites the parent rather than repeating it. Nothing here overrides a placement
rule; nothing there overrides a capture rule.

### What this book does not contain

No cameras, lenses, focal lengths, sensors, film stocks, apertures, shutter
speeds, sensitivities, meters or lighting equipment. No lighting diagrams. No
shot lists or shooting sequences. No quantities of images. No arrangement,
gallery, page or layout. No motion. No colour values, type, spacing or code.

Equipment and settings belong to production planning and change every few years.
Arrangement belongs to Phase 6. Motion belongs to Phase 5.

### The most important rule in this book

Before every exposure, and again before every publication:

> ## What evidence does this image provide?
>
> **If the answer is "none", the image must not exist.**

Everything else here is an elaboration of that sentence.

# PART I — PHOTOGRAPHY PHILOSOPHY

## 1. What an Elite Export photograph is

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 Photography philosophy · D1 Photography carries
the argument · Creative Direction Book §6, §11 · Documentary Storyboard §9

Brand Bible §16.1 states the position in five words: **photography is not an
illustration of the argument, it is the argument.** This chapter states what
follows for the person holding the camera.

### 1.1 The definition

> **An Elite Export photograph is a record of work happening in a building we
> own, made close enough to be examined and left honest enough to be checked.**

Four conditions, all required. Remove any one and the photograph becomes
something else — a stock image, a decoration, a thumbnail or a fabrication.

| Condition | Meaning | Removed, it becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A record** | It documents rather than proposes | A concept |
| **Of work happening** | An act, in progress, unarranged | A still life or a set |
| **In a building we own** | Ours, no exceptions, no libraries | Anybody's |
| **Examinable and unaltered** | Enough information to inspect; nothing removed | Advertising |

### 1.2 What a photographer is doing here

Not making pictures of a factory. **Producing the material a stranger will use to
decide whether to believe us.** The buyer's question — *is this a real factory, or
a trading house with a good website* (Brand Bible §6.2) — is answered by
photography more than by any other discipline, because it is the only discipline
that cannot be produced from an office.

The practical consequence: a photographer on this job is closer to a technical
witness than to an author. The authorship is real, and it is entirely in
**selection** — where to stand, when to press, what to leave in. It is never in
**arrangement**.

### 1.3 The register

Brand Bible §16.5: manufacturing is presented as **ordinary and exacting** — not
heroic, not romantic. The dignity is in the repetition.

An Elite Export photograph is therefore never impressive on purpose. It is
specific, plainly made, and correct. If a photographer's work in this library is
being praised for its beauty, something has gone wrong upstream of the praise
(Creative Direction Book §26.1).

### 1.4 The one-sentence test

> **Could this photograph have been obtained by somebody who does not work in this
> building?**

If yes, it is a picture of a factory. If no, it is evidence of ours. The whole
library is built from the second kind.

## 2. Why a photograph exists

**Traces to:** P1 Evidence over claims · P2 Restraint over decoration · Creative
Direction Book §6.3 · Documentary Storyboard §19

### 2.1 The existence test

Every image, at capture and again at publication:

> **What evidence does this image provide?**
>
> **If the answer is "none", the image must not exist.**

The answer must be a sentence, and it must be **specific**. *"It shows the
factory"* is not an answer; *"it shows that grading happens before any value has
been added"* is. A photographer who cannot complete the sentence has not yet found
the photograph.

### 2.2 Answers that do not count

| Non-answer | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| "It sets the mood." | Photography is never a mood-setter (Creative Direction Book §11.4) |
| "It's a beautiful frame." | Beauty is a by-product, never a brief (§6.3) |
| "It balances the composition." | An image used as a shape is decoration (§11.4) |
| "We need something here." | The need is for evidence; if none exists, the surface has one fewer image |
| "It shows leather." | Any competent photograph of leather would serve. Interchangeability is the definition of decoration (§11.4). |
| "It shows the team." | People are photographed doing, not being (Brand Bible §18.7) |

### 2.3 Fewer, larger, better

Restraint applies to a library exactly as it applies to a surface: **one strong
frame instead of three adequate ones** (Brand Bible §3.4). A library with fewer
images, all of which prove something, is stronger than a complete one where half
are filler — and it is much stronger than one where a single frame is borrowed
(Creative Direction Book §7.4).

### 2.4 The silent photograph is permitted; the empty one is not

A photograph may be published with nothing said about it (Brand Bible §12.4).
That is silence, and it is a confidence. A photograph that has nothing to say is
a different thing entirely, and it is deleted.

## 3. Visual truth

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §19 The Facts Register · §17.4 Material honesty ·
Creative Direction Book §6.2, §18.4 · Documentary Storyboard §19.5

### 3.1 The principle

> **A photograph is a factual statement, and the Facts Register governs it exactly
> as it governs a number.**

This is the sentence from which Parts III and IV descend. An image asserts: *this
happened, here, and looked like this.* Every one of those three can be falsified —
by staging, by borrowing, by editing — and each falsification is the same class of
offence as publishing an unconfirmed figure (Brand Bible §19.3).

### 3.2 The three truths a photograph must hold

| Truth | Falsified by | Governed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **That it happened** | Staging, re-enactment, demonstration, an act repeated for the camera | [§24](#24-authenticity-rules), Documentary Storyboard §9.3 |
| **That it happened here** | Stock, another company's floor, a borrowed detail, a generated image | [§24.2](#24-authenticity-rules) |
| **That it looked like this** | Removal, addition, composite, selective enhancement | [§21](#21-image-editing-philosophy) |

A photograph that fails any of the three is not a weaker photograph. It is a
false statement, and Creative Direction Book §25.5 classes it as unrecoverable:
once one image is known to be untrue, the viewer has no way to know which others
are.

### 3.3 Truth is not the same as flattery-free

A common misreading. Visual truth does not require that the work look poor,
cramped or difficult. The floor may be handsome; the light may be excellent; the
piece may be immaculate. **Truth is violated by alteration, not by good
fortune.** What is forbidden is producing the appearance rather than finding it.

### 3.4 The unannounced-visit standard

Documentary Storyboard §19.5 supplies the test and it is inherited here without
change:

> **A photograph is a promise about what a visit would find.**

If a buyer arrived unannounced on an ordinary Tuesday and could not recognise the
place, the bench, the state of the floor or the way the work is done, the
photograph is a set built on our own premises. Everything in
[§24](#24-authenticity-rules) exists to make that impossible.

## 4. Evidence and advertising

**Traces to:** Creative Direction Book §1 Look at work, not for sale · §6.1, §11.4
· Brand Bible §20.2 · Documentary Storyboard §20.3

The two categories look similar in a contact sheet and behave in opposite ways.
This chapter is the diagnostic.

### 4.1 The difference

| | Evidence | Advertising |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Made by** | Selecting | Arranging |
| **Its subject is** | What happened | What we would like understood |
| **Its detail is** | Incidental and specific | Chosen and representative |
| **Its condition is** | As found | As prepared |
| **Interchangeable?** | No. Only this frame proves this. | Yes. Another frame of the same subject would serve. |
| **Rewards** | Examination | Glancing |
| **Invites the viewer to** | Check | Admire |
| **Fails when** | It is too small to inspect | It is inspected |

### 4.2 The interchangeability test

Creative Direction Book §11.4 supplies the sharpest single test available and it
governs every selection made from a shoot:

> **If any competent photograph of the same subject would serve equally, the image
> is decoration.**

This is why *a hand stitching* is almost never a photograph and *this hand,
holding this piece, at this stage, with the bench and the room behind it* often
is. Evidence is specific or it is nothing.

### 4.3 Four things that convert evidence into advertising

Each is a small act, and each is complete on its own.

1. **Preparing the subject.** Clearing the bench, arranging the tools, positioning
   the piece. Evidence that has been tidied is testimony that has been coached
   (Creative Direction Book §6.2).
2. **Asking for the act.** A repeat, a pause, a hold. It becomes a demonstration
   (Documentary Storyboard §9.4).
3. **Improving it afterwards.** Removing a mark, a wire, a scuff, a person
   ([§21](#21-image-editing-philosophy)).
4. **Choosing for appearance.** Selecting the handsomest frame rather than the one
   that proves the most. This is the most frequent and least noticed of the four,
   because it happens in a quiet room weeks after the shoot.

### 4.4 What advertising photography looks like in this category

Recognisable at a glance and permanently unavailable to us: golden hour on
leather; a horse, a rider, a field; a product styled with props; a worker posed
with folded arms; a tool arranged at an angle; a machine photographed from below
to look formidable; a floor cleared before the camera arrived; shallow focus used
to make an ordinary object glamorous.

Every one of these is competent work. Every one would place us in the category we
declined to join (Creative Direction Book §1).

## 5. The evidence hierarchy

**Traces to:** Documentary Storyboard §10 Scene hierarchy, §19.2 Hierarchy of
narrative proof · Creative Direction Book §6

Documentary Storyboard §10 grades **scenes** by what they can carry in a story.
This chapter grades **photographs** by what they can prove on their own. The two
are related and are not the same: a story can build proof across scenes; a single
photograph has to carry its own.

### 5.1 The six ranks

| Rank | Kind | What it proves | Can it carry a surface alone? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **E1** | **A decision being taken** | That a standard exists and somebody applies it — grading, judging, refusing | **Yes.** The only rank that produces Recognition. |
| **E2** | **An act with its context** | That this work is done here, at this scale, by these people | Yes |
| **E3** | **A repeated act** | System rather than incident — the same operation, treated identically | Yes, as a group; no, singly |
| **E4** | **A record** | That the work was documented while it happened | No. Support only, and never early. |
| **E5** | **A located view** | That the place exists and is used | No. Carries place and silence. |
| **E6** | **An object alone** | Only its own condition | No. Specification, not argument. |

### 5.2 The rules

1. **E1 is scarce and is the point.** Everything in a production plan exists to
   put a photographer in front of an E1 moment. They cannot be arranged, only
   attended.
2. **No accumulation of E5 produces E2.** Twenty views of empty rooms prove a
   building and nothing else.
3. **E4 never appears before the work it records** (Documentary Storyboard N6).
4. **E6 is legitimate and limited.** A finished piece proves workmanship. It never
   proves a factory ([§10](#10-finished-work)).
5. **Rank is not quality.** A superb E5 is still an E5. Ranking governs what an
   image is allowed to be asked to do, not how good it is.

### 5.3 Where the ranks come from

E1 and E2 can only be obtained during a real shift, with access, by somebody
present when a decision happened. E5 and E6 can be obtained at any time by
anybody with a key. **The library's strength is therefore decided almost entirely
by access**, which is why access is the blocking item in
[§30.2](#30-dependency-notes-for-later-phases) rather than a logistical detail.

## 6. Place — how a factory is photographed

**Traces to:** Creative Direction Book §7.1 Interiors not exteriors, §7.2
Continuity of place · Brand Bible §3.1 Ownership · Documentary Storyboard §7.1 C1

### 6.1 From inside, always

An exterior proves nothing — anyone can stand outside anything (Creative Direction
Book §7.1). **Ownership is proved from the inside, repeatedly, from positions that
could only be obtained by somebody who works here.**

An exterior may exist for orientation. It may never be asked to prove ownership,
and it is never the strongest image in a set.

### 6.2 The room is a recurring subject

The most persuasive device available to this brand is **the same room appearing
again** (Creative Direction Book §7.2). A window recognised from an earlier frame.
A floor marking that recurs. A bench seen behind one operation and in front of
another.

The photographic consequence is a planning instruction, not a compositional one:

> **Photograph rooms enough times, from enough positions, that they become
> familiar.**

Familiarity cannot be produced by one excellent photograph of a room. It is
produced by accumulation, it is almost never consciously noticed, and it is the
reason a viewer stops doubting the building exists.

### 6.3 Scale is shown by recurrence, not by panorama

A single wide frame of the whole floor is a boast, and it reads as one. It also
fails the interchangeability test, because every factory looks like a factory from
far enough away.

Scale is better shown by the same space being entered repeatedly at working
distance — the viewer assembles the extent themselves, which is
[P4](#26-how-photography-serves-the-five-words) doing its work.

### 6.4 The place must look used

Documentary Storyboard §21.5. Offcuts, a scrap bin, a worn bench edge, a marked
floor, a wall that has been repaired. Creative Direction Book §6.2: leave the
unflattering in.

An immaculate floor reads as prepared, and prepared reads as untrue. **A
photographer who arrives to find a floor that has been cleared for them should
say so and wait**, because the photographs from that day will not be evidence.

### 6.5 Never

| Never | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Aerial or elevated survey of the site** | A camera on a crane reads as an advertisement (Creative Direction Book §7.3) |
| **The empty floor presented as the working one** | A fabricated fact (Documentary Storyboard §23.1) |
| **A room cleared, arranged or dressed** | §6.4 |
| **The building at golden hour** | Aspiration as fantasy (Brand Bible §7.2) |
| **Signage, logos or our own name as a subject** | Identities recede (D8). The building proves us; the sign does not. |
| **Another company's premises, in any part of any frame** | Unrecoverable (Creative Direction Book §25.5) |

## 7. Machine presence

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.6 Technology philosophy · §4.2 The central tension ·
Creative Direction Book §20 Technology, §25.2

### 7.1 Machines must appear

Not optional. Creative Direction Book §25.2 names the warning sign explicitly:
*only hands and tools appear; no machine, no room, no scale.* A library without
machinery drifts to artisan, undersells capacity, and breaks the central tension
(Brand Bible §4.2) — and a buyer needing four thousand pieces reads "artisan" as
"cannot".

### 7.2 A press is a press

Brand Bible §16.6: equipment is named plainly and never dramatised. The
photographic form of that rule:

- **In use, by somebody.** A machine's evidence is that it is running and that a
  person is responsible for it. An idle machine is E5 at best.
- **At working distance**, from where an operator stands — not from the floor
  looking up, which is the heroic register, and not from above, which is the
  inspector's (Creative Direction Book §7.3).
- **Whole enough to be identified.** A viewer who works in manufacturing should be
  able to see what kind of machine it is. That recognition is the evidence.
- **Plainly.** No detail study of a badge, a dial or a nameplate as an aesthetic
  object. That is machine-as-jewellery and it is the technology brochure's
  signature.

### 7.3 The relationship, not the object

The strongest machine photographs in this library are not photographs of machines.
They are photographs of **the decision around the machine**: the hand adjusting,
the eye checking the first piece off, the operator stopping it. Machinery
guarantees repeatability; hands decide how a piece ages; both are stated and
neither is celebrated (Brand Bible §16.6).

### 7.4 Never

| Never | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Low-angle heroic framing** | Drama. Awe is not our register (§16.5) |
| **A machine as sculpture** | Celebrates the equipment (§16.6) |
| **Anything that reads as futuristic** | Contradicts Permanence; reads as compensating for a lack of history (Creative Direction Book §25.2) |
| **Motion rendered as spectacle** | The blur of a fast machine is an effect, and effects date (§8.2) |
| **A machine we do not own or operate** | §24.2 |
| **Manufacturer nameplates presented as credentials** | Machine origins are unconfirmed under Brand Bible §19.4 and are not ours to claim as evidence |

## 8. Human presence

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 Camera at bench height · §18 People philosophy ·
§18.7 · Creative Direction Book §7.3 · Documentary Storyboard §23.2

### 8.1 People are photographed working

Brand Bible §18.7: photographed **working**, not posed; at the bench rather than
lined up. Never described with adjectives — and the photographic equivalent of an
adjective is a pose.

### 8.2 Bench height

Brand Bible §16.1 fixes this and it is not reinterpreted here: **the camera is at
bench height.** Looking down turns a worker into a subject being observed. Bench
height makes the viewer a colleague. This is the single most important
positional rule in the book, and it is also an ethical one — it is the difference
between documenting people and surveying them.

### 8.3 Where a person looks

> **A person may be photographed looking at their work, at another person, or at
> nothing. Not at us.**

Eye contact acknowledges that a recording is happening, which converts an observed
act into a performed one (Documentary Storyboard §9.3) and converts a photograph
into a portrait. Portraits are a different form with a different claim, and this
brand does not make them.

### 8.4 Hands are not the story

The artisan trap. A library heavy in hands is a library that has quietly changed
company (Creative Direction Book §25.2). Hands appear constantly and are almost
never the whole frame: the hand, the piece, the bench and the room behind it is
the photograph, because only the layered frame says *we have a craftsman inside a
working factory* (Brand Bible §16.1) — which is the question the buyer arrived
with.

### 8.5 Consent, dignity and attribution

| Rule | Source |
| --- | --- |
| No individual appears without their consent, asked before recording | Brand Bible §18.7 |
| Named individuals only with consent to be named | §18.7 |
| No error is photographed as somebody's error — the record finds the cause, not the culprit | §18.3 |
| No reprimand, dispute, injury, or moment that diminishes anybody | Documentary Storyboard §23.2 |
| Nobody is photographed doing the work in a way they would not normally do it — including without the protection they normally wear | Documentary Storyboard §14.4 |
| Nothing about a person's circumstances is photographed as content | §23.2 |

**Consent is not a release form obtained afterwards.** It is asked before, and a
photograph made without it is deleted rather than shelved.

### 8.6 Never

Posed groups. Folded arms. A person presenting a product to the camera. A person
mid-laugh for warmth. A model, or anybody who does not work here. A person used to
give a sense of scale. Anybody photographed from behind glass, from above, or
without their knowledge.

## 9. Material presence

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §17 Material philosophy · §17.2 The hide is a record ·
§17.4 Material honesty · Creative Direction Book §18.4, §18.5

### 9.1 Leather is photographed as a material, not as a luxury

The category photographs leather as an object of desire: warm, soft-focused,
glowing. That is the lifestyle ancestor we rejected (Creative Direction Book §1).

We photograph leather as what it is — a material with a grain direction, a density
gradient from backbone to belly, a thickness, an edge, a section and a memory
(Brand Bible §17.1). Those are physical facts, they are checkable by anybody who
knows the material, and they are what a buyer's technical colleague looks for.

### 9.2 What a leather photograph should make legible

- **Grain**, at its actual scale, unsmoothed
- **Thickness and section** — the cut edge is one of the most evidential surfaces
  on any piece
- **The state of the edge** — raw, bevelled, sanded, burnished; the stage is
  visible to anyone who works leather
- **Marks and variation** — [§11](#11-imperfection)
- **The relationship to the operation** — leather being worked, not leather
  arranged

### 9.3 Photograph it while it is being worked

A hide on a bench mid-operation is E2. The same hide arranged for the camera is
E6, and probably advertising ([§4.3](#4-evidence-and-advertising)). Where a
material study is genuinely needed — a section, an edge, a grain — it is made
**at the bench, in the room, in working light**, not brought somewhere.

### 9.4 Material honesty is a photographic obligation

Brand Bible §17.4 and Creative Direction Book §18.4 are binding on the camera:

- A split is photographed as a split, never captioned or shaded to suggest
  full-grain.
- An embossed grain is never presented as a natural one.
- A finish is what it is — pigmented, aniline, waxed, oiled — and is not
  photographed to imply another.
- Where a component is not leather, it is not photographed to imply it is.
- **A hide is never photographed, lit or graded toward uniformity.** Uniformity in
  this material is a warning sign (Brand Bible §17.2), and manufacturing it is
  manufacturing a fact.

### 9.5 Never

Leather we did not work. A hide borrowed to photograph. A material sample from a
supplier presented as our stock. Oil or water applied to make a surface read
richer. A surface polished for the frame. Anything that would make a
knowledgeable buyer's assessment of the material wrong.

## 10. Finished work

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.5 The process is the product · §12.4 Captions ·
Creative Direction Book §20 Products · Documentary Storyboard §22.2, §22.4

### 10.1 The finished piece is a receipt, not a payoff

Documentary Storyboard §22.2: a finished headstall resembles every other finished
headstall, and building to it is building to an anticlimax. In the library, the
finished piece is a minority and it is never the lead.

### 10.2 What a finished piece is evidence of

Not of a factory. **Of workmanship** — and only if it is photographed closely
enough to be inspected as workmanship rather than admired as a shape.

| Photograph it so that a buyer can assess | |
| --- | --- |
| Stitch density and regularity | Edge finish and consistency |
| How components sit where they overlap | Symmetry between paired parts |
| Hardware fit and alignment | The condition it actually leaves in |

An image of a finished piece that does not let a buyer assess any of these is
E6 at best and decoration at worst.

### 10.3 The condition rule

> **A finished piece is photographed in the condition it leaves the building in.**

Not cleaned beyond that, not conditioned for the camera, not selected as the best
of a batch and presented as typical. The piece photographed should be a piece that
shipped.

### 10.4 Where and how

Editorial, plainly: each piece given room, as a plate rather than a listing
(Creative Direction Book §20 Products). On a neutral ground or in the place it was
made. Nothing arranged with it — no props, no styling, no set, no context that
implies a use, a lifestyle or an owner.

Where text accompanies it, it is a specification label — *One ear headstall, dark
oil. Kanpur, 2026.* — never a sentiment (Brand Bible §12.4).

### 10.5 Confidentiality

Documentary Storyboard §22.4 is binding on every frame:

> **We show the process. We do not show the specification.**

A buyer's label, pattern, drawing, tolerance or unreleased design appears only
with their written permission, or the piece is photographed in a state that
identifies no label. A photographer who exposes one buyer's product demonstrates
to every other buyer what we would do with theirs. No deadline overrules this.

### 10.6 Never

A product styled as an object of desire. Props. A lifestyle context. A horse, a
rider, a field, or a horse silhouette (Brand Bible §20.3). A piece photographed in
use by a consumer. A group of pieces arranged to imply range. Studio light, which
implies the subject was brought somewhere to be photographed (Creative Direction
Book §14.2).

## 11. Imperfection

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §17.2 The hide is a record · §17.3 Variation in the
material, never in the work · §17.4 · Creative Direction Book §6.2, §18.4

### 11.1 Three kinds, three treatments

The distinction that decides most retouching arguments before they start.

| Kind | Examples | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Material variation** | Healed scars, insect and brand marks, growth wrinkles, tonal difference between hides, thickness variation | **Always kept.** It is the proof that the material is what we say it is (Brand Bible §17.2). Removing it is a fabricated fact (§17.4). |
| **Environmental wear** | A worn bench edge, a marked floor, offcuts, a scuffed machine, a repaired wall | **Always kept.** It is what makes the place look used ([§6.4](#6-place--how-a-factory-is-photographed)). |
| **Process failure** | Uneven stitch density, a mis-taken skive, an edge burnished in two passes instead of three, a width that varies | **Never published as passed work.** Not because it is unflattering — because it is a piece that failed the gate, and publishing it as finished work is an inaccurate claim about our standard. |

### 11.2 The line

Brand Bible §17.3: **material variation is honest; process variation is failure.**
A photographer who conceals the first is falsifying. A picture editor who
publishes the second as a finished piece is misrepresenting.

Both are the same error in opposite directions, and both come from treating
"imperfection" as one category.

### 11.3 Process failure may be photographed — as a rejection

This is the important exception and it produces some of the strongest images in
the library. A defective piece **being refused** is E1: it proves the threshold
exists, has a location, and costs something (Documentary Storyboard §21.2).

The rule is about the claim, not the subject: a failed piece shown as a rejection
is evidence; the same piece shown as output is a false statement.

### 11.4 The instinct to tidy

The most common well-intentioned failure on a shoot. Somebody moves the scrap bin.
Somebody wipes the bench. Somebody straightens the offcuts. Each is a small act of
kindness toward the photograph and a small act of damage to its value.

> **Nothing is moved for a photograph. If it was there, it stays there.**

The one permitted exception is safety, and safety always wins.

## 12. Distance philosophy

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 Camera at bench height · Creative Direction Book
§7.3 The camera belongs to a colleague · §6.2 Prefer the wider frame when it
contains proof

Distance is authorship. Where a photographer stands says who they are, and a
viewer reads it before they read anything in the frame.

### 12.1 The four distances and what they claim

| Distance | Reads as | Available to us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Removed** — the survey, the panorama, the elevated view | An inspector, or an advertisement | Rarely. Orientation only, never proof (§6.3) |
| **Colleague** — standing where somebody working nearby would stand | Somebody who belongs here | **The resting state.** Most of the library. |
| **Present** — close enough to see the decision, not so close as to interrupt | Somebody looking over a shoulder | Yes. Where E1 lives. |
| **Intimate** — the macro, filling the frame with a detail | A specimen under study, detached from place | Sparingly, and never as a lead image (Brand Bible §23.3) |

### 12.2 The default

> **Colleague distance is the resting state. Any departure from it must be
> argued.**

A library made mostly at intimate distance is an artisan library. A library made
mostly at removed distance is a corporate one. Ours sits in the middle two, which
is exactly where nobody else in the category stands, because standing there
requires being allowed in.

### 12.3 Prefer the wider frame when it contains proof

Creative Direction Book §6.2: a hand alone could be anywhere; a hand with a
working floor behind it is located. When choosing between two distances, the one
that keeps the evidence of place in the frame wins — unless the closer one is the
only way to make the decision legible, in which case the decision wins.

### 12.4 Distance and interruption

Present distance is the closest a photographer may work without the work changing.
The test is not aesthetic:

> **If the person being photographed altered what they were doing, the
> photographer was too close.**

That is a failure of access conduct (Documentary Storyboard §14.4), and the frame
is discarded regardless of how good it is.

## 13. Depth philosophy

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 Three planes in one frame · §4.2 The central
tension · Creative Direction Book §11.2 Immersion

### 13.1 Three planes

Brand Bible §16.1 fixes the structure and this book does not reinterpret it:
**foreground sharp** (hands, tool, leather taking the work), **midground soft**
(the bench, offcuts, thread), **background heavily blurred** (a second worker, a
machine, a window).

### 13.2 Why depth is an argument and not a style

This is the most important paragraph in Part III.

A factory panorama says *we have a building.* A macro says *we have a craftsman.*
**Only the layered frame says we have a craftsman inside a working factory** —
which is the resolution of the central tension (Brand Bible §4.2) and the question
the buyer arrived with.

Depth is therefore not an aesthetic preference. It is the compositional form of
the brand's central argument, and a flat frame has quietly declined to make it.

### 13.3 The consequence for selection

Brand Bible §23.3 already rules: a flat single-subject frame is **usable as a
detail, never as a lead image.** A library whose strongest images are all flat has
a structural problem no amount of quality will fix.

### 13.4 Separation, not obliteration

The background is blurred enough to recede and never so much that it stops being
identifiable as our floor. A background rendered to abstraction has thrown away
the proof it was there to carry — and it is also the shallow-focus glamour that
Creative Direction Book §3 forbids.

> **You should always be able to tell what the background is. You should never be
> reading it.**

### 13.5 Immersion

Creative Direction Book §11.2: an image that continues past the edge of the field
implies a world that continues past the edge of the frame. The capture-side
counterpart is that **frames should be made with extent** — composed so that the
place clearly continues outside them, rather than resolving neatly within them. A
frame that closes on itself is an object; a frame that opens is a view.

## 14. Framing philosophy

**Traces to:** D4 One idea per view · Creative Direction Book §16 Focus and eye
movement · Documentary Storyboard §9

### 14.1 One frame, one claim

A photograph makes one statement. Two subjects of equal weight in a frame produce
arbitration, and arbitration is friction (Creative Direction Book §16.1). Where a
frame contains two claims, it is two photographs, and usually one of them is
better.

### 14.2 What must be in the frame

Not a formula. A test:

> **Everything required to make the claim checkable, and nothing else.**

If the claim is *this decision is being taken here*, the frame needs the decision,
the hands, and enough of the room to locate it. If it needs the whole machine to
be identifiable, the machine is in. If a person's face is not part of the claim,
their face does not have to be in the frame — and their consent is still required
if they are recognisable at all.

### 14.3 Frame edges

The edge of a frame is a decision. Two rules:

- **Nothing important touches an edge accidentally.** An edge that clips something
  meaningful reads as carelessness, and a viewer reads carelessness faster than
  care (Creative Direction Book §2 Competence).
- **The edge may cut deliberately.** A frame that cuts through a bench, a machine
  or a room signals continuation and produces immersion ([§13.5](#13-depth-philosophy)).
  Deliberate cutting is authorship; accidental clipping is a mistake.

### 14.4 Level, square, and what they signal

Horizontals that should be level are level. A tilted frame is a technique, it is
datable, and it is the visual register of energy — which this brand does not have
(Creative Direction Book §8.2, Brand Bible §7.2).

Precision at the edges is how a viewer reads care long before they read anything
else (Creative Direction Book §2 Competence). This applies to a photograph exactly
as it applies to a piece of work, and for the same reason.

### 14.5 The frame is not fixed to a shape

No aspect ratio is prescribed here and none is prescribed anywhere: shape is a
placement decision and belongs to Phase 6. What this book requires is that a frame
be **made with enough around the subject that it can be placed later without
being violated** — see [§16.3](#16-negative-space) and
[§22](#22-cropping-philosophy).

---

## 15. Composition philosophy

**Traces to:** D5 Asymmetry with intent · Creative Direction Book §15, §16 ·
Appendix A.2, A.3

### 15.1 Composition is selection, not arrangement

The governing sentence of this chapter, and the line the whole book defends.

We compose by **choosing where to stand and when to press**. We never compose by
moving what is in front of us. Every compositional problem here has exactly two
solutions: change position, or wait. Moving the subject is the third, and it is
not available ([§11.4](#11-imperfection)).

This constraint is the reason our photographs look unlike the category's. It is
not a limitation to be worked around; it is the source of the difference.

### 15.2 Asymmetry is the resting state

Creative Direction Book §15.1: symmetry is the signature of a template. In a
photograph, a centred subject usually means the photographer stood where the
subject was rather than where the picture was.

Symmetry is permitted where the subject is genuinely frontal and formal — a
machine faced squarely, a rack of finished work — and it should be able to explain
itself (§15.2).

### 15.3 One entry point

Creative Direction Book §16.1: every field has one place the eye lands first, and
it should be the place we chose. In a photograph, the entry point is made by
**distance, depth and light**, not by arrangement — which is another way of saying
that all three chapters around this one are doing the compositional work.

### 15.4 Balance by weight

A large quiet area balances a small dense one (Creative Direction Book §15.3). In
a working frame, that usually means a person or a decision on one side and the
room on the other. An asymmetric frame that feels stable is the clearest evidence
of an author, and it is what separates a documentary photograph from a snapshot.

### 15.5 The system across unlike subjects

Creative Direction Book §9.2: the same logic of crop and framing applied to unlike
subjects. This is the Becher discipline (Appendix A.2) and it is what makes a
library read as one company rather than as several photographers.

**A viewer should be able to predict how the next photograph will be made.** They
should never be able to predict what it will contain.

## 16. Negative space

**Traces to:** D2 Silence is a material · §16.2 Whitespace philosophy · Creative
Direction Book §12

### 16.1 The distinction from Phase 2

Creative Direction Book §12 governs silence **around** a photograph, on a surface.
This chapter governs silence **inside** one.

They are different acts with the same purpose, and the second is the more urgent
of the two because of a simple asymmetry:

> **Space around an image can be added later. Space inside an image cannot.**

A crowded frame is permanently crowded. Whatever Phase 6 does with margins, it
cannot repair a photograph made without room in it.

### 16.2 What negative space does inside a frame

- **Isolates the claim.** A subject with room around it is examined; a subject
  surrounded by incident is scanned.
- **Communicates importance without enlargement** (Creative Direction Book §12.3).
- **Carries the held moment.** A photograph that asks nothing is the most
  compressed form of silence this brand has (Creative Direction Book §12.4).
- **Signals composure.** A frame that crowds is a frame that is worried you will
  look away (§16.2).

### 16.3 Room for placement

A practical instruction with no numbers in it: **frames are made with room around
the subject on more than one side**, so that a picture editor can place them later
without cropping into the evidence. A frame filled edge to edge is a frame with one
possible use.

This is not a request for empty photographs. It is a request that emptiness be
**allocated at capture** rather than hoped for afterwards — the capture-side form
of Creative Direction Book §12.2.

### 16.4 Negative space is occupied

In this brand, the space around a subject is almost never blank. It is floor,
bench, wall, room — quiet, unremarkable, and continuing to prove the place while
asking nothing. That is the ideal: **silence that is still evidence.**

### 16.5 The failure

Uniform emptiness. A photograph that is mostly nothing, with a small subject
somewhere in it, is not composure — it is an image that could have been made
closer and was not (Creative Direction Book §25.4, silence becoming padding).

## 17. Light philosophy

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 Warm daylight · Creative Direction Book §14.2
Working light, §14.4 Light and the passage of time

### 17.1 Working light

Creative Direction Book §14.2 fixes the position: **our light is the light the
work is actually done in** — directional, from a real source, at a real time of
day.

Brand Bible §16.1 fixes its character: **warm daylight. No mixed colour
temperature, no flash, no HDR, no heavy vignette.**

Neither is reinterpreted here. What follows is what they mean with a camera in
hand.

### 17.2 Why found light and not made light

| Made light says | Found light says |
| --- | --- |
| The subject was brought somewhere to be photographed | Somebody was already working here |
| A photographer decided how this should look | This is how it looks |
| An arrangement was made | An observation was made |

Studio light reads as commercial. Dramatic light reads as theatre and implies
interpretation. Working light is credible **precisely because it is imperfect**:
it falls unevenly, changes across a room, and produces exposures a studio would
reject. Those imperfections are the evidence that nothing was arranged.

### 17.3 The photographer works with the day, not against it

The consequences are practical and they should be planned for rather than
discovered:

- The floor's light changes through a shift; the same bench is a different
  photograph in the morning and the afternoon, and both are true.
- Some operations happen where the light is poor. They are still photographed. A
  dim frame that proves something outranks a bright frame that does not.
- Where a room genuinely cannot be photographed by its own light, the honest
  answer is that the room is photographed as it is, or not at all.

### 17.4 Light is how a brand says *when*

Creative Direction Book §14.4: anything lit as a current fashion will be legible
as that fashion within a decade. **Faithful, unremarkable, correctly exposed light
is invisible and therefore permanent.**

This is the single strongest reason to refuse a lighting style, and it is why this
chapter contains no look: a look is a date stamp on a twenty-year library.

### 17.5 Never

Flash. Mixed colour temperature. HDR. Heavy vignetting. Any light source
introduced to make a subject more attractive rather than more legible. Golden hour
used as a register. Practical lights arranged for effect. Anything that would make
a viewer aware of lighting.

## 18. Shadow philosophy

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 Deep shadows retaining detail · Creative Direction
Book §14.3 Shadow keeps its detail

### 18.1 The position

Creative Direction Book §14.3 states it as philosophy before technique, and it is
inherited unchanged:

> **Shadow conceals nothing. It is where the light happens to be less.**

A shadow that falls to pure black is a place where information has been discarded.
For a brand whose position is *you may examine us*, discarded information is a
small contradiction — and enough small contradictions are eventually felt as one
large one.

### 18.2 What that means at capture

Brand Bible §16.1: **deep shadows retaining detail, highlights held below
clipping.** Shadows may be deep — this is not a request for flat, evenly lit
frames, which would be studio light by another route. They must be **readable**.

The test is a viewer's, not a meter's: *could somebody who wanted to check what is
in that shadow do so?* If not, the frame withholds, and withholding is the one
thing this library may not do.

### 18.3 Shadow as composition

Shadow is a legitimate compositional instrument: it recedes what does not matter
and lets the eye enter where we chose ([§15.3](#15-composition-philosophy)). Used
that way it is doing the same job as depth.

It becomes a failure the moment it is used to **hide** — an untidy corner, an
unattractive machine, a wall that needs repair. Concealment by darkness is
retouching performed at capture, and the honest response to an unattractive part
of the floor is to photograph it or to leave it out of the frame.

### 18.4 Shadow and drama

Creative Direction Book Appendix A.7 names the reference we are closest to and
must not become: **Salgado's drama.** Magnificent, and it interprets. Our shadows
keep their detail because we conceal nothing.

Contrast exists to direct the eye and to separate importance. Any contrast that
draws attention to itself rather than to something has been misused (Creative
Direction Book §14.1).

## 19. Colour philosophy

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 No mixed colour temperature · §17.4 Material
honesty · Creative Direction Book §8.2, §8.3 Materials over effects

### 19.1 Colour is reported, not chosen

> **The brand's colour comes from the material, the light and the building. It
> never comes from a treatment.**

Leather, brass, thread, wood, painted machinery, a concrete floor, daylight
through a window. Creative Direction Book §8.3: where the brand needs richness, it
comes from what is being photographed, never from something applied afterwards.
Real material is permanent; applied effect is fashion.

### 19.2 Fidelity is a factual obligation

Colour is not an aesthetic layer on this library. It is **part of the factual
statement**, because a buyer assesses a hide's tone, a finish's character and a
dye lot's consistency by looking. A photograph whose colour has been styled has
misstated a material fact as surely as a caption that called a split full-grain
(Brand Bible §17.4).

The standard:

> **A photograph's colour should let a knowledgeable buyer form the same judgement
> they would form standing at the bench.**

### 19.3 Neutral, not sterile

Faithful colour is not the same as desaturated colour. Desaturation is a
treatment, it is fashionable in cycles, and it will be datable
([§8.2](#26-how-photography-serves-the-five-words)). Warm daylight is warm, and a
faithful rendering of it should look warm.

What is forbidden is a rendering that is **more** than faithful: pushed warmth,
lifted richness, a palette imposed across the library, a colour graded toward a
brand tone.

### 19.4 The relationship to Phase 6

Phase 6 will define a tonal system for the brand's surfaces. **That system does
not apply to photographs.** A photographic library graded to match a palette has
subordinated evidence to design, which reverses D1 and converts every image into
decoration.

Design accommodates the photographs. Photographs are never accommodated to the
design.

### 19.5 Consistency of colour across the library

Two hides may differ in tone and both be photographed truthfully. Two photographs
may not differ in **rendering** (Creative Direction Book §9.3). Variation in the
material is honest; variation in the work is failure, and rendering is our work.
See [§23](#23-the-single-grade).

### 19.6 Never

Mixed colour temperature. A colour cast introduced for warmth or coolness.
Selective saturation of a subject. Split toning. A monochrome conversion used to
lend seriousness — black and white is a treatment, and treating a factual library
is not available to us. Any grade that could be named as a look.

## 20. Texture philosophy

**Traces to:** D6 Material is felt, not seen · Creative Direction Book §18 ·
Brand Bible §17.2

### 20.1 Two opposite instructions, both correct

The distinction that prevents this chapter being read as a contradiction of
Creative Direction Book §18.

| | Applied texture | Photographed texture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What it is** | A material reference used as a graphic element on a surface | The actual grain, weave, wear and finish inside a photograph |
| **Governed by** | Creative Direction Book §18, D6 | This chapter |
| **The rule** | Sits at the threshold of perception. If a visitor can describe the motif, it is too strong. | **Fully legible.** It is the evidence. |

Applied texture is decoration pretending to be material. Photographed texture is
material. The first is suppressed; the second is the reason the photograph exists.

### 20.2 Texture is the most examinable evidence we have

Grain, stitch, edge, buff, the wear on a bench, the polish on a tool where a hand
has held it for years. These are the things a buyer leans in to look at, and they
are unfakeable at scale: a stock library can supply a beautiful hide, and it
cannot supply the specific wear pattern on our bench.

**Texture is therefore captured to be examined**, which means it must be rendered
with enough information to survive being looked at closely — the capture-side
counterpart of Creative Direction Book §6.1.

### 20.3 Texture is never enhanced

Sharpening, clarity, structure, micro-contrast, texture sliders: all of them
manufacture an appearance of material that the material did not have. An enhanced
grain is a rendered surface implying a natural one, which Creative Direction Book
§18.4 forbids in exactly those words.

If a texture does not read, the answer is to be closer, to wait for better light,
or to accept that it does not read.

### 20.4 Texture and the finished piece

On finished work, texture is where workmanship is assessed
([§10.2](#10-finished-work)). It is also where a shortcut would be visible, which
is precisely why we photograph it plainly and why the category photographs it
softly.

## 21. Image editing philosophy

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §17.4 Photography is never retouched to remove natural
marks · §19 The Facts Register · Creative Direction Book §18.4, §25.5

### 21.1 The principle

> **Editing may correct the recording. It may never correct the subject.**

A camera records imperfectly: it may misjudge exposure, misread the colour of a
room, or introduce artefacts of its own. Correcting those brings the image closer
to what was there. Everything else moves it away, and a photograph moved away from
what was there is a fabricated fact under Brand Bible §19.3.

### 21.2 Permitted

Each of these is a correction toward the truth of the scene, applied to the whole
frame:

- Exposure and contrast, toward what the room actually looked like
- Colour balance, toward the actual light ([§19.2](#19-colour-philosophy))
- Correction of a lens's own distortion
- Straightening a horizon that should be level
- Removal of artefacts introduced by the equipment — sensor dust, a flare from a
  dirty element
- Conversion, resizing and preparation for a medium, applied consistently

### 21.3 Forbidden

Each of these alters the fact, and each is unrecoverable in the sense of Creative
Direction Book §25.5:

| Forbidden | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Removing a mark from a hide** | A fabricated fact, named explicitly in Brand Bible §17.4 |
| **Removing anything from the floor** — a bin, a cable, a scuff, an untidy corner | Evidence that has been tidied is testimony that has been coached (§6.2) |
| **Adding anything** | Self-evident |
| **Compositing frames** — a better sky, a better face, a better hand | Two moments presented as one |
| **Selective adjustment to draw attention** — dodging a subject, darkening a background | Making a photograph argue what it did not show |
| **Cleaning or reshaping a person** | Nobody in this library is improved |
| **Removing a person** | Falsifying who was there |
| **Enhancing texture, clarity or micro-contrast** | Manufacturing material ([§20.3](#20-texture-philosophy)) |
| **Any generative or synthetic alteration, including fill, expansion and removal** | It invents content that never existed. There is no scale at which this is acceptable, including a single pixel. |
| **A stylistic grade, filter, emulation or look** | Datable and interpretive (Creative Direction Book §8.2) |

### 21.4 The retoucher's test

> **Would the person who was standing there recognise this as what they saw?**

If no, stop. Two supporting tests for borderline cases: *does this change what a
buyer would conclude?* and *would I be comfortable if this image were compared
side by side with the original file?* If the answer to the second is no, the edit
is a fabrication regardless of its size.

### 21.5 The original is never destroyed

Every published image is traceable to an unedited original, retained. This is the
photographic form of the Facts Register: **a number is traceable to the company
record; an image is traceable to a capture** ([§24.4](#24-authenticity-rules)).

An image whose original cannot be produced cannot be published.

## 22. Cropping philosophy

**Traces to:** Creative Direction Book §6.2 Prefer the wider frame when it
contains proof · §9.2 The same logic of crop applied to unlike subjects · Brand
Bible §16.1

### 22.1 A crop is an edit

It is the most common alteration in any library and the least examined, because it
removes nothing visible. It is still a change to the statement: a crop decides
what a viewer is permitted to check.

### 22.2 What a crop may do

- Remove an accidental edge intrusion
- Straighten
- Adapt an image to a medium, when the evidence survives intact
- Tighten onto a subject **whose context is proved elsewhere in the same set**

### 22.3 What a crop may never do

| Never | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Crop out the place** | The context is usually the proof. A hand alone could be anywhere (Creative Direction Book §6.2). |
| **Crop a three-plane frame into a flat one** | It discards the brand's central argument ([§13.2](#13-depth-philosophy)) |
| **Crop to remove an unflattering element** | Retouching by another name ([§21.3](#21-image-editing-philosophy)) |
| **Crop to manufacture a composition that was not made** | Composition is selection at capture ([§15.1](#15-composition-philosophy)) |
| **Crop past the point of examination** | Enlarging a small part of a frame produces an image with too little information to be inspected, which is decoration (Creative Direction Book §6.1) |
| **Crop a person out of a frame to change who was there** | Falsification |

### 22.4 The crop is decided once

An image has one canonical crop, decided when it enters the library, and every
medium uses it or uses the uncropped original. **Two different crops of the same
frame in circulation are two different statements**, and a library that permits
that has lost the consistency Repeatability depends on (Creative Direction Book
§9.2).

Where a medium genuinely requires a different shape, the correct response is a
different photograph — which is why frames are made with room in them
([§16.3](#16-negative-space)).

### 22.5 The default

> **Crop last, crop least, and prefer not to.**

A photographer who framed correctly leaves very little for a picture editor to do,
and that is the intended distribution of authority: composition belongs to the
person who was in the room.

## 23. The single grade

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 One grade across the whole library · §3.3
Repeatability · Creative Direction Book §9.1, §9.2

### 23.1 The rule

Brand Bible §16.1 and §3.3 are unambiguous: **one grade across the entire library,
shot to shot, whoever shot it and whenever.**

### 23.2 A grade is a rule, not a look

The distinction that keeps this rule alive for twenty years.

A *look* is a set of adjustments applied to make images resemble a style. It
dates, and applying one would forfeit Permanence
([§17.4](#17-light-philosophy)).

A *rule* is a definition of correct rendering: exposure judged the same way,
neutral colour judged the same way, shadows retaining detail, highlights held
below clipping. Two photographers following the rule in different rooms in
different decades produce compatible files. That is what is meant here, and it is
the Becher discipline applied to processing (Creative Direction Book Appendix
A.2).

### 23.3 Why it matters more than it appears to

A viewer moving through our work develops, without articulating it, an expectation
of how the next image will be treated — and being right is the argument (Creative
Direction Book §9.1). **A company whose own library is inconsistent is asserting a
process discipline it visibly does not have**, in front of a buyer whose entire
concern is process discipline.

An inconsistent library does not read as varied. It reads as several suppliers.

### 23.4 Material variation is not a grading problem

Two hides differing in tone are photographed truthfully and left different
(Creative Direction Book §9.3). Grading them toward each other is manufacturing
uniformity in a material where uniformity is a warning sign (Brand Bible §17.2).

**Variation in the material is honest. Variation in the rendering is failure.**

### 23.5 Governance

The grade is defined once, written down, and applied by whoever processes. It
survives a change of photographer, a change of agency and a change of technology.
Where a new medium requires a different preparation, the preparation changes and
**the grade does not**.

Any proposed change to the grade is a change to the whole library, retrospective,
or it is not made at all.

## 24. Authenticity rules

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §16.1 No stock, ever · §19 The Facts Register · §23.3
Photography decision tree · Creative Direction Book §25.5 · Documentary Storyboard
§14.4, §23

The régime that protects everything in this book. These rules are not stylistic
and are not subject to judgement.

### 24.1 It happened

- Photographed **during a real shift**, while the work was actually being done.
- **Nothing staged, arranged, posed or dressed.** A staged bench is visible
  instantly (Brand Bible §16.1).
- **No act repeated for the camera.** An act performed once is not performed again
  for the record; a second attempt is a re-enactment (Documentary Storyboard
  §14.4).
- **Nothing done differently because a camera is present** — including faster,
  more carefully, and without normal protection.
- **Nothing moved for a photograph** ([§11.4](#11-imperfection)). Safety is the
  only exception.

### 24.2 It happened here

- **Ours, taken in our facility. No exceptions, no libraries, no "temporary
  placeholder" in published work** (Brand Bible §23.3).
- **No stock photograph, anywhere, at any size, in any context.** A single stock
  image will undo the credibility of everything else (Brand Bible §16.1), because
  the viewer then has no way to know which other images are ours.
- **No generated or synthetic imagery**, whole or partial. An image of a factory
  that never existed is the purest possible violation of a brand built on
  ownership, and the fact that it may be undetectable makes it worse rather than
  better.
- **No borrowed detail** — another company's machine, hide, product, floor or
  building, in any part of any frame.

### 24.3 It is ours to show

- **Consent before presence** for every recognisable individual
  ([§8.5](#8-human-presence)).
- **Written permission for any identifiable buyer's product, label, pattern or
  design**, or the piece is photographed in a state that identifies no label
  ([§10.5](#10-finished-work)).
- **Nothing that would let a competitor reconstruct a buyer's specification.**

### 24.4 It can be traced

The photographic form of the Facts Register, and the rule that makes the other
three enforceable twenty years from now:

| Requirement | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **The unedited original is retained for every published image** | An image that cannot be produced in its original state cannot be defended ([§21.5](#21-image-editing-philosophy)) |
| **Capture date and location are recorded and kept** | An image's claim includes *when* and *where*; an undated library cannot answer either |
| **The photographer is recorded** | Authorship is accountability, and this brand's whole position is that somebody is answerable (Brand Bible §1) |
| **Consents and permissions are held on file against the images they cover** | A consent that cannot be produced is not a consent |
| **An image with no provenance is not published, however good it is** | Unverifiable is unpublishable, exactly as it is for a number (§19.2) |

### 24.5 The standing instruction on placeholders

The most likely route by which this régime fails, and it fails quietly.

> **There is no such thing as a temporary image.**

A borrowed photograph used to develop a design will eventually be seen, and the
person who sees it will not know it was temporary. Where our photography does not
yet exist, the correct response is **no image**, and the surface is designed
around its absence.

### 24.6 The three that end the argument

The photographic form of Creative Direction Book Appendix B.4. Not disagreements
about craft — Brand Bible violations, which no deadline, budget, client preference
or aesthetic judgement may overrule.

> **A stock or generated image, anywhere, at any size.**
> **A retouched hide.**
> **A staged act presented as observed.**

If you are being asked for one of these, the conversation is no longer about
photography.

## 25. The library is the argument

**Traces to:** Creative Direction Book §2 Reassurance, §7.2 Continuity of place,
§9 Repeatability · Documentary Storyboard §7, §25

A single photograph proves an instant. **A library proves a company.** Most of
this brand's photographic power is a property of the collection rather than of any
image in it, which means the collection has to be planned rather than accumulated.

### 25.1 What a library must contain

Not a quantity — a coverage. Four requirements, and a library missing any one of
them has a structural gap that no individual image repairs.

| Requirement | Why | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Decisions** | Recognition is bought only with decisions being taken (Documentary Storyboard §10.1). A library without them is a library of activity. | E1 |
| **Every chapter of the story** | The ten canonical chapters (Documentary Storyboard §7). A chapter with no photography cannot be told in any medium. | E1–E4 |
| **The same rooms, repeatedly** | Continuity of place is the primary proof of ownership (Creative Direction Book §7.2) | E2, E5 |
| **The same act, more than once** | Repeatability, argued rather than claimed (Creative Direction Book §9) | E3 |

### 25.2 Coverage is planned against the chapters, not against the pages

The library is planned against Documentary Storyboard §7's chapter set, because
that set is medium-independent and pages are not. A library covering all ten
chapters can serve a documentary, a catalogue, a trade show, a recruitment piece
and a website without a further shoot. A library planned against a set of pages
will need reshooting the first time anything is redesigned.

### 25.3 The weakest image sets the ceiling

A library is judged the way a batch is judged. One borrowed frame invalidates the
collection (Creative Direction Book §7.4); one styled product image invites the
viewer to reconsider the others; one inconsistently graded file suggests the rest
were graded by chance.

**It is better to have fewer images, all ours and all consistent, than a complete
set of mixed provenance.** This is the same standard the company applies at its
own gate, applied to itself.

### 25.4 A library ages differently from a design

A design will be replaced. This library, if it is made correctly, will outlast
several. That asymmetry is the reason for every refusal in Part III and Part IV:
a look applied in 2026 will have to be lived with in 2046, and by then it will be
the only thing anybody notices.

> **Photograph for the archive, not for the launch.**

## 26. How photography serves the five words

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §3 Brand DNA · Creative Direction Book §5–§9

The Brand DNA is the measure of every decision in every discipline. This chapter
states what each word demands of a photographer, and what the failure looks like.

### 26.1 Ownership

**Demands:** interiors, repeatedly, from inside; the same rooms recurring; our
people, our equipment, our light; colleague distance; nothing borrowed at any
scale.

**Produces:** the involuntary acceptance that this building exists — which is the
one thing no competitor can obtain, because obtaining it requires owning a floor
to stand in (Creative Direction Book §7).

**Fails when:** exteriors carry the proof; a library is all details and no rooms;
any frame contains something that is not ours.

### 26.2 Evidence

**Demands:** the existence test on every frame ([§2.1](#2-why-a-photograph-exists));
specificity over beauty; the wider frame when it contains proof; the unflattering
left in; nothing removed afterwards.

**Produces:** a buyer's ability to check rather than believe — the whole position
of the company in a single discipline.

**Fails when:** an image could be swapped for another photograph of the same
subject without loss (Creative Direction Book §11.4).

### 26.3 Repeatability

**Demands:** one grade across the library; the same framing logic applied to
unlike subjects; the same act photographed more than once; consistency of
rendering across photographers and decades.

**Produces:** the answer to the buyer's actual fear, argued by our own conduct
rather than claimed about our floor.

**Fails when:** the library reads as several photographers; when treatment varies
to keep things interesting (Creative Direction Book §9.1).

### 26.4 Restraint

**Demands:** fewer frames, larger; one claim per frame; negative space allocated at
capture; the silent photograph; the willingness to publish no image rather than a
weak one.

**Produces:** the composure that reads as a company with nothing to prove
(Creative Direction Book §10).

**Fails when:** images are made to fill; when a set includes three adequate frames
instead of one strong one.

### 26.5 Permanence

**Demands:** no look, no grade fashion, no processing style, no technique
traceable to a year; faithful light; materials rather than effects; framing and
structure rather than treatment.

**Produces:** a library still usable in 2046, which is the actual commercial
objective of this document.

**Fails when:** a knowledgeable person could name the decade the photographs were
made in (Creative Direction Book §8.1).

### 26.6 Recognition — what the words are for

Recognition is not one of the five words. It is what the five words together
produce, and it is the hinge the whole brand exists to reach (Brand Bible §8.1
Stage 3).

Documentary Storyboard §20.2 sets the four conditions and they are inherited here
as a photographic brief:

| Condition | What a photographer looks for |
| --- | --- |
| **Specific** | A particular decision about a particular thing, not a general practice |
| **Unglamorous** | Something nobody would have chosen for how it looks |
| **Costly** | A judgement with a price — a refusal, a slower route, material set aside |
| **Consequential** | Something a viewer can see will matter later |

And the rule that follows from Documentary Storyboard §20.3, which is the most
useful single instruction a photographer on this job can carry:

> **Photograph the judgement, not the virtuosity.**

Skill photographs as admiration, and admiration is the artisan register. A
decision photographs as recognition, and recognition is the sale.

## 27. When a photograph is rejected

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §23.3 Photography decision tree · §24.2 Before
publishing any image · Creative Direction Book §23, §24

Brand Bible §23.3 is the governing tree and runs first, unchanged. The trees below
extend it into the decisions this book adds. **A failure at any point stops the
image; later branches do not rescue it.**

### 27.1 Any photograph

```
Did we take it, in our building, during real work?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT. No exceptions, no libraries, no
│                                generated content, no placeholders (§24).
└─ Yes
   │
   Can you complete the sentence "this image proves ___"?
   ├─ No ─────────────────────► DELETE. It must not exist (§2.1).
   └─ Yes
      │
      Would any competent photograph of the same subject prove it equally?
      ├─ Yes ─────────────────► Decoration. REJECT (§4.2).
      └─ No
         │
         Has anything been removed, added, or improved (§21.3)?
         ├─ Yes ──────────────► REJECT. A fabricated fact.
         └─ No
            │
            Does it match the single grade (§23)?
            ├─ No ────────────► Reprocess. Do not publish as-is.
            └─ Yes ───────────► PROCEED to the subject tree
```

### 27.2 A photograph containing a person

```
Have they consented, before the exposure was made?
├─ No ────────────────────────► DELETE. Not shelved (§8.5).
└─ Yes
   │
   Are they working, or presenting?
   ├─ Presenting (posed, arranged, looking at us) ──► REJECT (§8.1, §8.3)
   └─ Working
      │
      Is the camera at bench height?
      ├─ No ──────────────────► REJECT. Above is an inspector; below is
      │                          theatre (Brand Bible §16.1).
      └─ Yes
         │
         Did they change what they were doing because of the camera?
         ├─ Yes ──────────────► REJECT. Too close (§12.4).
         └─ No
            │
            Does the frame diminish them in any way (§8.5)?
            ├─ Yes ───────────► DELETE
            └─ No ────────────► PROCEED
```

### 27.3 A photograph of a finished piece

```
Is it in the condition it leaves the building in?
├─ No (cleaned, conditioned, styled for the frame) ──► REJECT (§10.3)
└─ Yes
   │
   Is anything arranged with it — a prop, a set, a context?
   ├─ Yes ────────────────────► REJECT (§10.4)
   └─ No
      │
      Is a buyer's label, pattern or unreleased design identifiable?
      ├─ Yes ─────────────────► Written permission on file?
      │                         ├─ No ──► REJECT (§10.5)
      │                         └─ Yes ─► Continue
      └─ No
         │
         Can workmanship actually be assessed in it (§10.2)?
         ├─ No ───────────────► It is E6 decoration. Reshoot closer.
         └─ Yes ──────────────► PROCEED
```

### 27.4 A photograph of the material

```
Is the grain rendered as it is, unenhanced and unsmoothed?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT (§20.3, §9.4)
└─ Yes
   │
   Has any natural mark been removed or reduced?
   ├─ Yes ────────────────────► REJECT. A fabricated fact (Brand Bible
   │                             §17.4). Unrecoverable.
   └─ No
      │
      Would a knowledgeable buyer misjudge the material from this frame?
      ├─ Yes ─────────────────► REJECT (§19.2)
      └─ No
         │
         Was it photographed at the bench, during work?
         ├─ No ───────────────► It is a studio still life. Reject or
         │                       reshoot in place (§9.3).
         └─ Yes ──────────────► PROCEED
```

### 27.5 A photograph being considered as a lead image

```
Does it read at three planes (Brand Bible §16.1)?
├─ No ────────────────────────► Usable as a detail. Never as a lead
│                                (Brand Bible §23.3).
└─ Yes
   │
   Is it E1 or E2 (§5.1)?
   ├─ No ─────────────────────► A view cannot lead. Find the decision.
   └─ Yes
      │
      Is the place identifiable in it?
      ├─ No ──────────────────► It could be anywhere. REJECT (§12.3).
      └─ Yes
         │
         Is there room in the frame for it to be placed without cropping
         into the evidence (§16.3)?
         ├─ No ───────────────► Usable, but constrained. Prefer another.
         └─ Yes ──────────────► PROCEED
```

### 27.6 An edit or a crop

```
Does the change bring the image closer to what was there?
├─ No ────────────────────────► REJECT. Editing corrects the recording,
│                                never the subject (§21.1).
└─ Yes
   │
   Is it applied to the whole frame, or selectively?
   ├─ Selectively ────────────► REJECT (§21.3)
   └─ Whole frame
      │
      Would the person standing there recognise the result (§21.4)?
      ├─ No ──────────────────► REJECT
      └─ Yes
         │
         If cropping: does the place survive the crop (§22.3)?
         ├─ No ───────────────► REJECT
         └─ Yes
            │
            Does the unedited original exist and is it retained (§24.4)?
            ├─ No ────────────► Cannot be published, at any quality.
            └─ Yes ───────────► PROCEED
```

### 27.7 Anything not covered

Apply the existence test ([§2.1](#2-why-a-photograph-exists)), then Brand Bible
§23.3, then §24.6. If the image survives and no tree covers it, it may proceed,
and this chapter should be extended in the next version.

## 28. Photography failure modes

**Traces to:** Creative Direction Book §25 · Documentary Storyboard §27

Each failure carries its **early warning sign** — visible on a shoot or in a first
selection, before the failure is built into a library. Catching one early costs a
conversation; catching it late costs a reshoot, and some of these cannot be
reshot.

### 28.1 Category failures — the library becoming somebody else's

| Failure | What it looks like | Breaks | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The artisan library** | Hands, tools, close detail; no room, no machine, no scale | Repeatability, the central tension | Somebody says the hand images are the strongest work |
| **The corporate library** | Wide surveys, tidy floors, posed staff, an exterior lead | Ownership, Evidence | The floor was cleared before the shoot |
| **The product catalogue** | Finished pieces on neutral ground, in quantity | Evidence | Finished-piece frames outnumber process frames |
| **The lifestyle library** | Golden light, styling, context, aspiration | All five | A prop appears |
| **The technology brochure** | Machines as sculpture, low angles, detail studies of dials | Permanence | A machine is photographed with nobody near it |

### 28.2 Truth failures — the image is no longer a fact

| Failure | Mechanism | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The tidied frame** | Something was moved before the exposure | Anybody straightens anything |
| **The repeated act** | An operation performed again for the camera | Somebody says "can you do that once more" |
| **The improved file** | A mark, a cable or a bin removed afterwards | A retoucher asks whether something is "distracting" |
| **The borrowed frame** | Stock, generated, or another company's | An image appears that nobody can date or locate |
| **The undated file** | Provenance lost | An image is in circulation whose original cannot be found |

### 28.3 Craft failures — true, and not evidence

| Failure | Mechanism | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The flat frame** | One subject, no planes; proves a craftsman or a building, never both | Lead candidates are all close-ups |
| **Too far** | The survey; every factory looks like a factory from far enough away | The place cannot be told from a competitor's |
| **Too close** | The specimen; detached from place, and the work altered by the photographer's presence | Subjects glance at the camera |
| **The crowded frame** | No room inside it; unusable, uncroppable, unexaminable | Every frame is filled edge to edge |
| **The concealing shadow** | Darkness used to hide rather than to recede | A frame is praised for being moody |
| **The interchangeable frame** | Any photograph of the same subject would serve | The caption could be attached to a different image |

### 28.4 Library failures — the collection, not the image

| Failure | Mechanism | Early warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Inconsistent rendering** | Several grades in circulation | Two images of the same room do not match |
| **Chapter gaps** | A canonical chapter with no photography | A telling cannot be made without a reshoot |
| **No decisions** | Activity without judgement; nothing is E1 | Nothing in the library changes a sceptic's mind |
| **Two crops of one frame** | The same statement made two ways | A picture editor re-crops for a new medium |
| **The placeholder that stayed** | A temporary image published | Anybody uses the phrase "just for now" |

### 28.5 Integrity failures — unrecoverable

These do not weaken the library. They end its argument, and no later work repairs
them.

| Failure | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **A stock or generated image, at any size** | The viewer has no way to know which other images are ours |
| **A retouched hide** | A fabricated fact (Brand Bible §17.4) |
| **A staged act presented as observed** | Every other frame becomes questionable |
| **Another company's floor, machine, hide or product in any frame** | The ownership claim collapses |
| **A person published without consent** | A breach the company cannot defend, on any grounds |
| **A buyer's design published without permission** | Demonstrates to every other buyer what we would do with theirs |
| **A published image with no retained original** | Unverifiable, and therefore indefensible when questioned |

> Every failure in §28.5 is a Brand Bible violation before it is a photographic
> failure, and no craft judgement may overrule one.

## 29. Photography QA checklist

**Traces to:** Brand Bible §24.2 Before publishing any image · Creative Direction
Book §26 · Documentary Storyboard §28

Applied to every image before it enters the library, and to the library as a whole
before release. **Pass/fail.** Any single failure returns the image; a review is
not scored.

### 29.1 The one question

> ## What evidence does this image provide?

If the answer is "none", the image must not exist. Delete it — do not archive it,
do not keep it for a background, do not hold it as a placeholder.

### 29.2 Per image

**Provenance**
- [ ] Ours, taken in our facility?
- [ ] Made during real work, unstaged, unarranged, unrepeated?
- [ ] Was anything moved before the exposure?
- [ ] Is the unedited original retained, with date, location and photographer?
- [ ] Has every recognisable person consented?
- [ ] Is any buyer's product, label or design identifiable without written permission?

**Evidence**
- [ ] Can you complete "this image proves ___" with a specific sentence?
- [ ] Would any competent photograph of the same subject serve equally?
- [ ] What rank is it (E1–E6), and is it being asked to do more than its rank allows?
- [ ] Is there enough information in it to be examined closely?

**Frame**
- [ ] Does it read at three planes, or is it flat?
- [ ] Is the place identifiable?
- [ ] Is the camera at bench height where a person is present?
- [ ] Is there one claim, or two?
- [ ] Is there room around the subject for it to be placed later?
- [ ] Is anything important clipped by an edge accidentally?

**Rendering**
- [ ] Is the light the room's own?
- [ ] Do the shadows retain detail? Are highlights held?
- [ ] Is the colour faithful to the material as it would be judged at the bench?
- [ ] Does it match the single grade?
- [ ] Has texture, clarity or micro-contrast been enhanced?

**Truth**
- [ ] Has anything been removed, added, composited or selectively adjusted?
- [ ] Has any natural mark on a hide been reduced in any way?
- [ ] Does the crop preserve the evidence, and is it the canonical crop?
- [ ] Would the person standing there recognise this as what they saw?

### 29.3 Per library

- [ ] Is every canonical chapter covered (Documentary Storyboard §7)?
- [ ] Are there E1 images — decisions being taken — or only activity?
- [ ] Do the same rooms recur often enough to become familiar?
- [ ] Is the same act photographed more than once, treated identically?
- [ ] Does the whole library read as one photographer, whoever shot it?
- [ ] Are machines present, or has it drifted to artisan?
- [ ] Is there a single image in it that is not ours?
- [ ] Could a knowledgeable person name the decade it was made in?
- [ ] Would this library still be usable in twenty years?

### 29.4 On failure

Return the image with the failed question named. Do not negotiate the question and
do not accept an exception on grounds of deadline, access, cost or beauty.

**A brand built on restraint is destroyed by exceptions, not by mistakes**
(Creative Direction Book §26.3). A rejected image is deleted rather than kept for
a lesser use — a library that holds failed images will eventually publish one. If
a reviewer believes a question here is wrong, the remedy is to amend this book in
a new version, not to pass the image. Where an image is published against this
book, record it: the danger is never the first exception, it is the second, which
cites the first.

### 32.6 Tensions examined and resolved

| Apparent tension | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| Creative Direction Book §18 puts texture below the threshold of perception; §20 here demands it be fully legible | §20.1 — applied texture is a graphic element and is suppressed; photographed texture is the evidence and is legible. Opposite instructions, different objects, both correct. |
| Creative Direction Book §6.3 permits beauty; §1.3 here says an image is never impressive on purpose | Beauty as by-product is permitted; beauty as brief is not. Inherited unchanged from §6.3. |
| §3.3 says truth is not violated by good fortune; §4.3 forbids choosing for appearance | Finding a handsome frame is selection; preferring it over a frame that proves more is the failure. The test is what the frame proves, not how it looks. |
| §11 keeps imperfection; §11.1 forbids publishing process failure | Material variation is honest, process variation is failure (Brand Bible §17.3). §11.3 permits process failure to appear **as a rejection**, which is evidence, never as passed work, which is a false claim. |
| §17 demands found light; some operations are poorly lit | §17.3 — a dim frame that proves something outranks a bright frame that does not. Introducing light would forfeit the credibility the chapter exists to protect. |
| §19 demands faithful colour; Phase 6 will define a tonal system | §19.4 — the tonal system governs surfaces, never photographs. Design accommodates the images. |
| §16 asks for room in the frame; §2.3 asks for fewer, larger, stronger images | Not in conflict: room around a subject is compositional, not distance. §16.5 names the failure of a small subject in a large emptiness. |
| §22 permits cropping; §15.1 says composition is selection at capture | §22.5 — crop last, crop least, prefer not to. Authority over composition stays with the person who was in the room. |
| §24 forbids all borrowed imagery; design work needs images before a shoot | §24.5 — there is no such thing as a temporary image. The surface is designed around the absence. |
