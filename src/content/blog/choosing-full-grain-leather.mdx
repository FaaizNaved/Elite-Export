"Full-grain" is the most abused phrase in leather sourcing. Here is how to check a sample
yourself before a purchase order commits you to a container of it.

## 1. Look at the edge, not the face

A cut edge on genuine full-grain shows a dense, tightly packed fibre structure that gets
looser toward the flesh side. A pigmented finish sitting on top of a sanded surface reads as
a distinct plastic-looking layer.

## 2. Find the flaws

Full-grain keeps its history: insect bites, healed scars, growth lines. A hide with a
perfectly uniform face has almost certainly been corrected and embossed.

## 3. Press it with a thumbnail

Full-grain briefly holds the mark and then recovers. Heavily finished leather either shows
nothing at all or takes a permanent scratch through the coating.

## 4. Smell it

Vegetable-tanned leather smells of tannins and wood. A strong chemical or plastic smell
usually means a heavy acrylic topcoat.

## 5. Check the water drop

A drop of water on unfinished full-grain darkens and absorbs slowly. On a corrected,
pigmented surface it beads and sits.

## 6. Ask for the tannery

Any manufacturer working with certified tanneries can name them. If the answer is vague, the
supply chain is worth a second look.
